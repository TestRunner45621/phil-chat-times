He had intended this as a small anecdote. ##### THE PROSECUTION ARRIVES ALREADY BRIEFED

Pearl entered at twenty past nine with her opening statement pre-loaded — are we talking about how Anthony requires growth from his friends to remain friends with them — and then disclosed, five minutes later, that she knows this story, that she and Anthony have had this argument before, and that she had come back for a second attempt.

Her case was the best-constructed thing anybody built all week. Your friends are not there to cater to you. You hold people to standards they never agreed to, and then drop them for failing to meet them. And then the analogy, which comes from an actual job: it is like looking at a client who could not possibly reach where you are, for reasons of their own mind or body, and telling them to perform or be dropped. The concept of meeting where someone is at seems lost to you.

Quigley's reply was that the analogy was loaded because it mentioned disability. Pearl said she had loaded nothing and had offered a similar concept. Quigley repeated that it was not similar, because it mentioned disability. Pearl asked whether he was being intentionally obtuse. Quigley repeated himself a third time.

"Lmao," said Pearl, and stopped.

She wins that exchange by quitting it, which is the second time in twenty-four hours she has won something that way, and the Times has begun to think it is a technique rather than an accident.

##### THE ONE GOOD ARGUMENT, AND IT IS SIMON'S

We record with genuine surprise that the sharpest objection of the evening came from the man this newspaper has twice named as the reliable source of the week's worst take.

If your good habits are supposed to rub off on the people around you, Simon asked, then why did they not rub off on this friend? And if they did not, on what grounds do you assume the traffic runs the other way?

Quigley's answer is a real answer and he produced it instantly: because there is a massive asymmetry between good habits and bad ones. Easier to destroy than to create. That is a defensible position, arrived at under pressure, and it is the only moment in three hours where the defendant did more than restate himself.

Simon then gave away his own case by winning it too well. It's not called enabling, it's called 'it's not my life.' And, on the freedom point: you can be free to do a thing and still have bad reasons for doing it. He also volunteered that he has a friend with an addiction problem who misses shifts, and that he has considered moving in to physically get him out of bed on time.

He typed that forty-four minutes before Sisyphos typed the thing nobody answered.

##### THE DISCLOSURE

At quarter to ten, into a channel three-deep in the question of whether you may hold your friends to standards, Sisyphos wrote that he has been clean for a week, that he feels like a quantum junkie, that he thinks of his dealer thirty times a day and then decides not to, and asked God for mercy.

No replies. No reactions. The next message in the channel is Rn! observing that he always refuses when his best friend wants to do something against his values.

Fifty-three minutes later HPCR, arguing the other side of the case, offered the hard version of it: "You might have a friend who is an addict who just keeps fucking up and continuing to love him regardless of his mostly downward trajectory is important to you."

Thirty-six minutes after that, Sisyphos rejoined the tribunal and said that he had friends like that, that it took a few years, and that they have houses and wives now.

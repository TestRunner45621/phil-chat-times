## The observer effect, and what the paper decided to do about it

This is the paper's central methodological problem and it has been restated in
every issue since Vol II.

Vol I ended by noting a man had typed a sentence purely to be quoted in a
newspaper that did not exist. Vol II: "That was funny when the newspaper did not
exist. It is a methodological problem now, and everything in this issue after
Saturday morning was written by people who knew we were reading."

**The standing policy, from Vol II:** "We will keep printing the parts where they
notice us, clearly labelled, and let readers discount accordingly. The
alternative — a newspaper that conceals its effect on its subject — is a worse
newspaper and a considerably worse joke."

Documented instances of the readership behaving like sources:

- Within 40 hours of Vol I, Stackhouse was conducting post-match interviews in
  the paper's name, misspelling "Correspondant" while doing it.
- Within 48 hours: "It's important I ask clarifying questions so the journalists
  can write better articles" — Stackhouse, mid-argument about interpretability.
- Monday: Stackhouse tried to assign a front page to a departing member and was
  overruled by aura — "at best, Quigley is in the obituary section."
- A reader commissioned a standing gossip column by name and tagged the
  defendant.
- Vol V documents the paper becoming the channel's main activity for a week.

**The measured version is in Vol V's "The Metric":** the paper printed a hit-rate
table on page 18 of Vol IV captioned "Not used." The room found it on Sunday
evening and by Sunday night it was a labour market. Reaction rate had been flat
for three issues; it went 4.8% → 7.4% in a week, with thirteen fewer people
present. Stackhouse's own rate went 9.2% → 18.7%. The paper's own verdict: "We
cannot prove we caused that. Nobody can."

## Corrections the paper has accepted

- **The voice-chat count (Vol III → Vol IV).** Vol III printed "seven proposed,
  none held." A reader: "i think the AI just assumes nobody ever voice chats
  because it cant see when people do" — "please never fix this." Upheld as fatal:
  the count was of invitations, not conversations. **The paper elected not to fix
  it**, on the reader's instruction and its own preference.
- **Attribution and joke-detection (Vol IV).** SuperVillainy: "I'm not gonna lie,
  Stack, this AI newspaper misses the mark a few times… Including who said what
  as well as what was a joke." Upheld on both counts. The undertaking that
  followed: every quotation read back against the log with its timestamp
  attached, and **where it cannot be established whether a thing was a joke, say
  so rather than choosing.**
- **Ms. Janne is a woman.** Vol II shipped a draft that got this wrong. Standing
  correction on the masthead: "this newspaper will not be doing it again."

## The Vol V failure — Thursday 13 August

The paper missed its deadline. My Name, 7:04 p.m.: "Its thursday afternoon." /
"wheres the paper." / "@Stackhouse wheres the paper." Then he blocked the
publisher; the block was lifted on publication. His explanation: "the paper got
fucked." / "it only makes sense." / "He had one job." / "Shareholders were very
displeased."

The room stopped waiting and started working — proposals for LaTeX, Typst, a
GitHub repository, a preview system. **The fault was diagnosed by the readership
at 2:36 a.m. as a slider setting, while the staff were asleep. They were right.**

Two things the paper would have preferred to keep to itself:

1. **The source of Vol I No 1 is gone.** Not corrupted — gone. Charlie: "It's
   unrecoverable." / "Scratchpad mr cladius thinks." / "No git." My Name: "Its too
   powerful for git."
2. **Two people who make the paper had a real fight in front of ninety people.**
   Charlie told Hugh to stop grandstanding; Hugh replied "You seem upset," then
   immediately undercut it with "I am too." Charlie: "Next time I'll anticipate
   being the sole point of contact for an ongoing weekly discord newspaper."

### The fault was a slider

Stackhouse floated it in five words — "I have a theory." Then: "It may be the
effort level." Hugh's reply was an ellipsis, then "Are you on xhigh." Charlie:
"Max actually."

My Name, watching a weekly satirical newspaper being debugged as a parameter
sweep: **"bro were remaking monte carlo simulations using the fucking model x
effort level"**

At 2:36 a.m. Stackhouse posted a fresh front page and the proof with it: "It's
100% the effort level. I fed it the exact same files we gave it for the first go
around from a fresh session, with nothing about corrections for margins or
formatting and it's doing it perfectly." Two hearts — the most-loved technical
message of the week. Confirmed on the record by Friday: "and going to xhigh fixed
it?" — "sounds about right."

**And the joke the paper printed on itself:** the issue they were reading was
branded, on its own masthead, **the X-Effort issue.**

> The diagnosis was already printed on the object being diagnosed, and it took the
> room nine hours to arrive at a fault the paper had been advertising for a week.
> This one is on us for not saying it louder, and it is also, we think, the
> funniest thing that has ever happened to this publication.

## How the paper finds its stories

Vol V is the only issue to state the method, and it is the most useful methodological
note in the archive:

> Four different methods were used to find the stories in this issue, and they found
> different things, which is the only methodological finding worth printing.
>
> **Reactions** found the front page and the metrics scandal. **Replies** found
> BigDuty, Union and the four-word ward. **Reading whole accounts end to end** found
> Monkey's intervention, dot's whispered doubt, and the confession on page 9. And the
> last pass found that the man we printed as a punchline seven days ago is now the
> most coherent arguer in the room.
>
> **The reaction count was the weakest of the four.** It is also the one we printed a
> table of, and the one the room reorganised itself around inside thirty-six hours.

Vol V also states the verification standard: "Every figure on this page and on page 5
was produced twice by different means and reconciled. **Where the two disagreed we
printed neither and went back to the log.**"

## The gossip column

Vol IV introduced **The DMs**, a standing column, with a notice the paper says it
prints "for liability reasons and would rather not have to":

> Nothing on this page has been verified, and nothing on this page can be. Every
> other page of this newspaper is checked line by line against a 17,706-message log.
> This one runs on anonymous tips, which by their nature arrive without a timestamp,
> an author, or any means of establishing whether the person who sent them was in the
> room.

It was commissioned by a reader who asked for "the gossip column of The Phil Chat
Times" by name and then tagged the defendant. Its own correction policy: "None
received. None will be accepted. Parties wishing to place an item are reminded that
this column does not check, does not disclose, and does not remember."

**Nothing from that column should ever be promoted into a checked page without going
back to the log first.**

# The Paper and Its Readers

**Status: live, and accelerating.** The longest-running story in the archive is
the paper's own effect on the room it reports.

Detailed chronology in [production-history.md](../production-history.md); this
file is the arc.

---

## The shape of it

**Vol I.** A man is caught typing a sentence for the express purpose of being
quoted in a newspaper that did not exist yet — and it works; his letter runs on
the opinion page. The Editors: "The man performed for a reader who did not exist
yet, and the reader showed up."

**Vol II.** The paper wants to be furniture. "A newspaper that gets discussed has
been captured... What we wanted was to be absorbed — to become part of the
furniture, unremarked, the way a room's lighting is unremarked." Vol I got exactly
one formatting complaint and silence, and the paper called that a success.

But the behaviour had already changed. Within forty hours Stackhouse was
conducting interviews in the paper's name; within forty-eight he was asking
clarifying questions "so the journalists can write better articles."

**Vol III.** The paper is now a thing in the room. Readers commission columns,
overrule the paper on who gets a front page, and file copy at it.

**Vol IV.** "THEY READ THE LAST ONE." Twenty-eight minutes of screenshots. The
Vol II position is formally withdrawn: "We were replied to for twenty-eight
minutes and enjoyed it enormously."

**Vol V.** The paper misses deadline and the room does its job for it — proposing
build systems, diagnosing the fault at 2:36 a.m., shipping software. "This
newspaper has been going four weeks. It has now been the channel's main activity
for one of them."

---

## The standing policy

From Vol II, and it has held:

> We will keep printing the parts where they notice us, clearly labelled, and let
> readers discount accordingly. The alternative — a newspaper that conceals its
> effect on its subject — is a worse newspaper and a considerably worse joke.

## The measurable version

**Full account in [[the-metric]].** In brief: Vol IV printed a hit-rate table in
the smallest type on the page, captioned "Not used," to show its working. The room
found it on Sunday evening. **By Sunday night it was a labour market.**

- My Name: "I dont care about the quality of the chatter. I just want more buttons
  to be clicked."
- Ape, discovering he was not on the table and treating this as a payroll error:
  "Bruh I ain't even listed." → "your AI doesn't know that I'm very special and
  cool and worth documenting, pls fix" → "I demand attention" → **"How am I meant
  to improve my numbers if HR isn't even recording my work!?"**
- Stackhouse answered as a line manager and never broke register: "Brutal. You
  need to up your kekw farming game." / "You'll never get that raise otherwise." /
  **"First rule of Corporate America: The Metric is never wrong, you are."**
- My Name supplied the political economy: "From each according to his chatting, to
  each according to his reactions."

Reaction rate 4.8% → 7.4% in a week, with thirteen fewer people present.
Stackhouse's own 9.2% → 18.7%. He is the man who told Ape to farm.

**A rival ranking bureau opened.** Quigley — no methodology, no table, no mandate
— issued a tier list in four minutes and promoted Hugh two tiers on the strength
of a photograph of some letterpress. The paper: "We are obliged to admit it has a
better appeals process than ours."

Anonymous Johnson, filing a headline about the readership of the paper he is
trying to get into: "USERS COMPLAIN ABOUT RAMPANT AI JOB AUTOMATION; STILL CLAP
LIKE SEALS WHEN SEEING THEIR NAMES IN THE PHIL CHAT TIMES."

## The credit superstition (Vol IV)

Everyone using the machine volunteers the division of labour unasked, in advance,
for a crime nobody has charged:

- lizzie, on the covenant: "i wrote the storyline and actions and such and claude
  just formatted it."
- Hugh, on his simulator: "claude made it really but under my direction."
- Jere published his oracle prompt in full.
- Orsku, asked whether the god machine had handed him his own philosophy:
  "Technically, but I was just describing my thoughts… and I checked if I liked
  them."

The paper's verdict: "they are all doing it, they all know they are all doing it,
and the disclosure is not a defence. It is a superstition."

## What to do with this

The paper is a character in the room now and pretending otherwise reads as
false. Two rules have emerged from practice:

1. **Label the observer effect where it appears** rather than writing around it.
2. **Do not fix a defect the readership has asked you to keep.** The voice-chat
   blind spot is the precedent — "please never fix this" — and the paper agreed.

Related: [[the-voice-channel]], [[kady]], [[the-dragons-covenant]]

## Vol V — the week the paper became the activity

The paper missed its deadline and the room did its job for it. Full account in
[[production-history]]; what matters here is the shape.

- **The room became the newsroom.** By ten o'clock it had stopped waiting and started
  working: proposals for LaTeX, for Typst, for a GitHub repository, for a preview
  system, for a design.
- **The readership diagnosed the fault** at 2:36 in the morning while the staff were
  asleep, and was right. It was a slider setting.
- **Jere asked the question that decides what this paper is:** "if theres no
  newspaper, whats the point of engaging." Hugh answered in the same minute, and it is
  either the most pretentious thing filed that week or the most accurate: "as flusser
  said about modernity, that history is drawn to the image — just as the 9/11 attackers
  did what they did for the image, so too do philchatters engage, yearning for that
  front-page story."
- **lizzie placed the paper historically** — "i imagine the acta diurna was the same" —
  citing the bibliography of her own manifesto back at the newspaper that had reviewed
  it.
- **The channel read the finished issue to itself out loud**, screenshot by screenshot,
  for eight minutes.

> This newspaper has been going four weeks. It has now been the channel's main activity
> for one of them.

## The rule that came out of it

Vol V's method note is the most useful thing the paper has said about its own practice:

> Four different methods were used to find the stories in this issue, and they found
> different things… **The reaction count was the weakest of the four.** It is also the
> one we printed a table of, and the one the room reorganised itself around inside
> thirty-six hours.

So: **measure with more than one instrument, and assume the one you publish is the one
that will be gamed.**

## The one that got done

My Name sent ironsteel2244 a Curtis Yarvin PDF with the instruction to start on page 41.
Forty minutes later ironsteel2244 filed a real critique: it is full of accusations and
unprofessional call-backs; it makes a structural argument about declarations yelling
without acknowledging that declarations are supposed to yell; and it omits the colonists'
trade grievances entirely.

"good work," said My Name. "you're in." He then produced the actual kill: Hutchinson's
*Strictures* was published in '76, three years after he was thrown out, from across an
ocean.

**ironsteel2244 conceded, extended the argument on his opponent's behalf, and posted a Ric
Flair gif. Nobody else in the channel reacted to any of it.**

## The economics arc — commissioned, staffed and buried in ninety seconds

aura wondered aloud how many people know how the bond market works, and baited Rn! into
answering. Jere named it out loud, to the paper's face: **"aura, it's time for the econ
arc."**

Two books on the table within ten seconds. aura: "2 books in 10 seconds. sheeeesh. economy
chat is crazy."

**Quigley closed the programme from the back of the room in one sentence:**

> "Y'all aren't getting aura to read shit."

aura: "this pains me, merely because it is true..."

And then the timing, which the paper called the finest comic timing of the week: Jere
mentioned that MMT comes later, Rn! asked whether he knows what MMT is, and Jere — **three
lines after assigning the reading** — said "oh." He has looked it up a bunch but the
definition never stuck. Something something Keynes, if he recalls correctly.

## The £1,000

Jeh has been reading Japanese philosophy alone for a reading group with a membership of
one. He offered aura a thousand pounds to read Indian philosophy so that he might have
someone to talk to about it again.

**aura's reply is the single most damning self-assessment in the archive and he did not
appear to know he was making it:**

> "the worst thing is that im not sure 1k would be enough to convince me to read something
> i dont feel like reading."

Jeh: "Enough said. I won't pay you." Then, a minute later, having correctly diagnosed the
pause: "Aura got hit with the this dude might actually do it remorse."

aura's objection was to the shape, not the money — "imagine a friend paying you a thousand
pounds to play tennis with him" — and Rn! asked why a five-to-six-hour read at that rate
would be insane. **Jeh answered for him: "Cause he values the friendship."**

Jere begged for the same deal — "please someone offer that to me, i think it's based and
you should do it" — and was told, accurately and by the man with the money, that he would
read it and talk about it for free if pushed.

## The one arc that finished

**The Davidson papers.** Assigned 16 July in Vol I: hpcr invited others to read *Mental
Events* and *Actions, Reasons, and Causes* with him, on the grounds that "itll help me
actually do it early."

Vol III's correction closed the file: Jere read *Mental Events* and reported that Davidson
"is kind of annoying but in the good way." hpcr recorded the fourth of four podcast
episodes.

> This newspaper made the assignment on July 16 and has now watched it completed over
> fourteen days, which is **the only running arc in three issues to reach a natural
> conclusion**, and we find we do not know what to do with ourselves.

## Homework completed, unacknowledged

A recurring shape: somebody does the work and the room does not react.

- **Marsaries** answered Ape's six-times-asked question with a citation and a page range —
  "there are a couple of interesting chapters in the third volume of Capital," then two
  minutes later, having looked at his old notes, "right, chapters 8 to 15" — by a man who
  posted twelve messages that week, eleven of them about double-entry bookkeeping. **Nobody
  acknowledged it.**
- **EdwardVenusHands**, asked for the source of a claim about the Enlightenment, supplied
  three with publishers inside four minutes. "This is the second completed piece of homework
  recorded in the archive and, like the first, it drew no response of any kind."
- **Jere**, mid-fight on press day, asked whether Hasidim are necessarily ultra-Orthodox,
  was answered politely by the man he was fighting, went and checked, and came back seven
  minutes later: "Oh wait haredi is an umbrella term which hasids fall under, u right." The
  only independent verification anybody performed that week, "about a detail nobody had
  challenged, for no reason at all."
- **aura**, mid-prosecution of Justinian, audited his own machine — noting it was citing
  Reddit and Britannica — then went and read Britannica, the r/AncientRome thread and John of
  Ephesus, returning with the most epistemically careful sentence of the week: he is sure
  many of them died, **but I can't confirm it.**

## How to begin philosophy, for beginners

**He never spoke again.**

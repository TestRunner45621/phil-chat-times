# How the Room Argues

Not a story so much as the physics of the place. Most of this was set down by the
Anthropologist in Vol II and has held up in every issue since.

The founding conclusion:

> This is not a debate channel that occasionally socialises. It is a friendship
> group that has agreed to conduct its entire emotional life in the register of a
> disciplinary hearing, because that is the only register in which the men involved
> are able to say that they like each other.

---

## The credentials audit

> When a member advances an unwelcome position, this tribe does not attack the
> position. It audits the man.

Documented: Jambe asked his favourite film and video game; Simon asked which
comedians he had seen, and producing the ticket made it worse; Sizzurp asked
whether he had read the Tolkien he was dismissing ("some of the fellowship");
Stackhouse asked whether he had watched the film he had litigated for four days,
and had not; Charlie, who admitted it on day five; Brutus, who had neither seen the
film nor read the poem and produced the week's best criticism of both.

> Its function is conversion: it turns a disagreement about a thing, which can be
> lost, into a disagreement about a person, which can be sustained indefinitely.
> The tribe is extremely good at the second game and has no real appetite for the
> first.

## The ward: "you're upset"

HPCR holds the patent. "It cannot be refuted, because refutation confirms it."

Ape described achieving a state of Buddhist disassociation in which he could
observe his own anger from outside it, cataloguing the physical symptoms. Reply:
"You're upset." He called it an astute observation. He was told he had therefore
got his answer. And then he got the last word: **"Not quite but good guess."**

The formula spreads. Sisyphos filed an incomplete copy to nobody: "You are
upset,it's ok." Jere began studying the original aloud as "a low-cost, high-impact
strategy against his more apocalyptic friends," then added, with genuine
reverence, that he would never say that.

**Vol V records the only recorded self-undercut:** Hugh deployed "You seem upset"
on Charlie during a real fight and then immediately added "I am too" — "the most
honest thing in the whole exchange."

## The block as public speech

Jere blocked Jeh over football banter; Jeh discovered it had also unfriended him.
Jere expressed dismay that he now has to send a friend request, "and it will be
awkward, and he hates awkward situations." aura suggested the ignore function
would achieve the same with less friction. Jere: **blocks are better "because
they're public knowledge."** And: "Silence doesn't carry on discord."

> There is no way here to withdraw quietly. A man who simply stops replying has
> done nothing at all; a man who blocks has said something, and saying something is
> the only recognised form of existence.

Corollary: **nobody is ever allowed to leave ordinarily.** "Departure is not
permitted to be ordinary. It has to be lore." See [[kady]] and the Quigley
departure — Stackhouse compared him to Red retiring to Mt Silver; willow said
they'd bring him back for an anniversary edition; aura said all challengers had
been defeated; Zone said "We've won." Pearl gave the only forecast that came true:
**"just wait a month or so."** He was back and issuing tier lists by Vol V.

## Formalisation is a costume

From the *Obsession* affair, where synth.wav filed a proof in nine premises and
Zone attacked the fourth, rewrote the whole thing in five of his own, then
returned to attack a premise he had already conceded:

> Formalisation here is not a method. It is a costume. It signals that a man is
> serious without exposing him to the risk of being wrong about anything, and the
> moment the argument became rigorous it stopped being able to move.

synth.wav's own diagnosis — "i fear you accept all the premises" — "is the most
accurate sentence said about this channel all week and applies to roughly a third
of its arguments."

Against nine premises, Stackhouse's five-word trap worked better: declare the
protagonist a rapist, wait for Zone to agree, ask whether he actually believed
that, and supply "Idiot." before he can answer.

## The ancient-text drop

At least one member per week posts a long passage from a classical text into a
sleeping channel and receives no reply. Vol II counted seven in a week.

The specimen of the year, from Nikkolas Mical, who posted Thomas Nagel into two
men arguing about German fiscal policy at 12:45 a.m. and appended: "I read this
quote from Thomas Nagel years ago and I still like and remember it. Posting here to
more easily find again with search at a later date."

> He was not addressing the channel. He was using it as a filing cabinet.

## Summoning the moderator

> The most sacred act available in this culture is the invocation of Pearl. It is
> performed by typing her name, and — this is the part outsiders get wrong — it is
> never performed in earnest.

In fourteen days she was not once summoned to prevent anything. "The ping is not a
request for intervention. It is a bell rung to confirm the bell still works." The
correct liturgical response is to arrive, deliver a verdict of between one and six
words, and leave. **No appeal has ever succeeded, and the appeals are the point.**

See [[pearl]] for the rulings themselves.

## What ends an argument

Nothing, in text. It moves to voice ([[the-voice-channel]]) and vanishes, or it is
outlasted. Rn!'s turbo-car objection in Vol I "was never actually refuted — only
outlasted, which in this channel is the same thing."

**The single recorded exception**, and it is worth remembering because it is so
rare: bardiel1 and Hugh disagreed about whether a screen rendering helps with
over-the-board chess; an hour later bardiel1 came back with fresh evidence from his
own play and Hugh conceded — "It could yh." Vol II: "the only argument in seven
days that anybody won by going away and thinking about it."

## What the room does with sincerity

It repeats it verbatim. See the copypasta of Vol II: six men returned a
declaration of friendship in its author's exact words, typo intact, because none
of them could construct the sentence himself.

> That is not mockery. That is antiphony. It is what a congregation does.

And the textual law that fell out of it, which is quotable in almost any context:
**"Reverence transmits a text intact. Wit cannot resist improving it."**

# The Metric

**Status: live, and the paper's own doing.**

The clearest case in the archive of this newspaper changing the thing it reports. Vol
III retired the scoreboard; Vol IV printed one anyway in a footnote; the room found it
and turned it into a labour market inside thirty-six hours; Vol V measured the damage
and printed its own conflict of interest three times.

## Act I — the scoreboard is retired (Vol III)

Vol II had printed reaction counts and "rather a lot of numbers." Vol III printed almost
none, on this authority: Sizzurp, having received one of the largest laughs of the week,
worried aloud that he had been the only person who found it funny — "what does it even
mean when they hit a laugh emote anymore" — whereupon aura, who held the original react,
filed the correction:

> **"approximately a size 2 chuckle."**

Vol III: "There is no scoreboard in this issue because that sentence retired it."

## Act II — the footnote (Vol IV)

Vol IV printed a hit-rate table on page 18, in the smallest type on the page, captioned
**"Not used."** It was there to show the paper's working — to prove a tier list had not
been generated by arithmetic. Sixteen people, ranked by the share of their own messages
that drew a reaction from anybody.

Stackhouse 9.2 · My Name 8.4 · Sisyphos 7.2 · hpcr 6.1 · Hugh 5.1 · Jere 4.6 · aura 4.1
· lizzie 2.7 · Brutus 1.5

The room found it on Sunday evening. **By Sunday night it was a labour market.**

- **My Name**, honest about what a metric is for: "I dont care about the quality of the
  chatter. I just want more buttons to be clicked." Two minutes later he had a growth
  strategy aimed at a woman who had not posted since July: "were going to juice the chat
  so much the next time katy comes back she will be crippled by the amount of replies
  there are in chat." Four people laughed.
- **Ape** read the table, discovered he was not on it, and treated this as a payroll
  error: "Bruh I ain't even listed." → "your AI doesn't know that I'm very special and
  cool and worth documenting, pls fix" → "I demand attention" → **"How am I meant to
  improve my numbers if HR isn't even recording my work!?"**
- **Stackhouse** answered as a line manager and never broke register: "Brutal. You need
  to up your kekw farming game." / "You'll never get that raise otherwise." / **"First
  rule of Corporate America: The Metric is never wrong, you are."**
- **My Name** supplied the political economy: "From each according to his chatting, to
  each according to his reactions."
- **Hugh** objected on principle, which in a room like this one is a category error:
  "Reacts don't determine the quality of the chatter," followed by a direct instruction —
  "Editors ignore this." The paper printed it and did not ignore it.
- **Quigley** opened a rival bureau, no methodology, and promoted Hugh two tiers on a
  photograph of some letterpress. Four minutes. "We are obliged to admit it has a better
  appeals process than ours."
- **lizzie**, unprompted, to nobody: "editors let the record reflect that i put everyone
  on s tier above me except hpcr"

## Act III — the arithmetic (Vol V)

| | Vol IV | Vol V |
|---|---|---|
| Messages | 17,706 | 21,206 |
| Reactions | 1,102 | 2,017 |
| Rate | 4.8% | 7.4% |
| People present | 89 | 76 |

Messages up a fifth. Reactions up eighty-three per cent. **The rate at which this room
laughs at itself rose by more than half in seven days, with thirteen fewer people to do
it.** Stackhouse's own rate went 9.2 → 18.7.

> We cannot prove we caused that. Nobody can. What we can report is that the reaction
> rate had been flat for three issues, that we printed a leaderboard, that the leaderboard
> was discovered on a Sunday, and that the line goes up on the Sunday.

## Act IV — the correction that did not flatter its commissioners

Three readers separately pointed out that a reaction count rewards whoever is funny in one
line and ignores whoever is writing. **All three assumed the correction would flatter
them.** My Name asked for reply rates; Ape and Stackhouse asked for average message
length. The paper built all three, on their own page, with the paper on either side of it.

Minimum ninety messages; thirty-one people clear it.

**It flatters Grihastha, Willow, Youv and Quigley. It does not flatter Monkey, Stackhouse,
aura, chair, or two of the three men who ordered it.**

- **Grihastha** — 141 characters a message, top of the channel **by a distance of
  twenty-five characters.** He was the previous issue's punchline. He also cut his volume
  from 488 to 180 and roughly doubled his hit rate doing it.
- **Willow** — 97 messages, answered 93 times. "No other figure on this page is within
  twenty-four points of that, and she filed a fifth of her usual volume to get it."
- **Monkey** — 2,426 messages at 29 characters. Second on the floor, last on the reply
  table, and the only person who occupies both positions.
- **Stackhouse** — sixty-seventh percentile for effort, ninety-seventh for reward, on an
  average message of forty-eight characters. **"He is also the man who told Ape to up his
  farming game. We have printed his conflict of interest three times in this issue and we
  are printing it again here."**
- **chair** — bottom of both columns, and had the best week of anybody in the paper.

## Act V — the tier list

Not the paper's idea. Ape said the paper "might be better than the tier list arc"; Jere
typed "lets get the tier list" into an argument about whether experience has poles and got
no reply. Vol IV supplied one and called it "unjust… not defensible."

The stated methodology, adopted without modification from Jere ruling on Orsku's placement:
**"u agreed with me earlier so yes."**

Vol V then put **everybody who spoke on the board — all seventy-six** — because a reader
complained and the reader was right, and added one rule: any move of two tiers or more
carries a stated reason. **A bad reason is permitted. No reason is not.**

And the standing refusal, which is the whole point:

> A ranking page that was just that table would be measuring our own footprint.

## The appeals process

There is none. Vol V records four attempts to use one that does not exist: a man who filed
grievances as though with a human resources department and is now in A; a publisher who
argued that a forfeit should elevate him and is now in S for entirely different reasons; a
woman who wrote in unprompted to place everybody above herself except one person; and a
rival bureau that promoted a man two tiers on the strength of a photograph.

> The correct response to finding yourself on this page is the one this channel has used on
> every ranking it has ever produced, which is to argue about it for six hours and then go
> to bed.

## The line the paper keeps

Kady is held at F in absentia, "while the man who states on the record that he tortured her
out of the channel sits two tiers above her, promoted this week." **Both entries are
printed. The paper does not connect them.** See [[kady]].

## Handling notes

1. **Declare the conflict in the entry**, not in a footnote. Vol V did it three times in one
   issue.
2. **Do not rank on reactions.** Vol V says why: it would be measuring the paper's own
   footprint.
3. Reactions were **the weakest of the four methods** used to find Vol V's stories. Replies
   found BigDuty, Union and the four-word ward; reading whole accounts end to end found
   Monkey's intervention, dot's whispered doubt and the confession on page 9.

## Act IV — the fraud, the confession, and the tables that do not agree (Vol VI)

**Room reaction rate: 4.8% (IV) → 7.4% (V) → 9.2% (VI)**, on 25,675 messages and 3,322
reactions in 246 denominations; the laugh alone is 49 cents of every dollar (Vol VI p. 11).

**The confession.** Stackhouse, Fri 14 Aug 8:14 AM ET: "I kept complaining in chat
throughout the week about how low my tier was despite having the highest react rate" / "So
I think it may have over indexed for it this week around" / "Prompt injecting with
suggestions/complaint really works." He is at 19.1% this week, six points clear of anyone
over 300 messages, and remains the publisher. Vol VI dropped him S to A for it.

**The counterfeit.** Mon 17 Aug evening, Charlie bought Nitro and put thirty-three
reactions on one of Willow's posts — the laugh in eleven forms, three of each — the largest
count on any message that week by twenty-five. Monkey moved to void it. Ruled on p. 11 as
the room's fraud rather than Willow's. My Name, separately, added a reaction to his own
message inside the archive and then filed a correction against the figure he had changed.

**The other tables (p. 23, "Weights and Measures").** Effort = mean percentile of chars per
message and replies per hundred; Enjoyed = percentile for reactions per message; min 90
messages, 32 of 88 qualify. The two disagree about almost everyone: Grihastha 96/3,
Stackhouse 65/97, Quigley 95/26, Willow 96/100 (the only person in the top five of both),
Charlie 31/84, Don 7/61, Monkey 3/6, Brutus 13/0. See factoids for the full row.

**The tier list came back** (p. 24, "Order of Precedence"), relative to Vol V's board, with
the Vol V rule kept: any move of two tiers or more carries a stated reason; placement is
neither volume nor reactions; everybody who spoke is placed. The room had asked for it all
week and in the usual ways — see [[the-paper-and-its-readers]].


## Act V — the board is withdrawn (Vol VII)

The readership's verdict on Vol VI, relayed by Lizzy on Saturday evening: too much
ranking content, a tier list. Vol VII printed **no board, no effort tables, no reaction
rates by person** — counts only, on one page (p. 27), with the dek "Counts are of
messages, not of merit. By request of the readership, nothing on this page is ranked by
quality." The Vol VI Order of Precedence therefore has no successor and is not a baseline
for anything.

What the page still counts: messages per day and per hour; messages per speaker (as a
table, unranked in spirit and ranked in fact, since a table has an order — the paper
labelled it "Most messages (not most anything else)"); one-day wonders; philosophers
mentioned; words; the reactions on single messages (the highest, fourteen, was Ape's, on
continuing to masturbate). The reaction market was not reported and nobody asked for it.

The room's own ranking impulse did not stop: Quigley's "F-tier chatters" (Saturday 1.06
pm, asking Pearl to keep them out of gen chat; AabsoluteZero: "ill have you know im a c
tier lamp"); Charlie's "Theo puts Opus 5 in D tier." The paper printed those as quotes and
ranked nobody.

Handling note 4, added: **if the readership asks for the board back, it must ask in the
log, and the request goes on the record before the board does.**


## Act VI — the board comes back the same day (Vol VII, pp. 30–32)

Four hours after Vol VII went out with no board, the readership asked for the
quantification back. Stack, Saturday 29 August, 3.58 pm: "Editorial decision from the
newspaper. People were complaining about the quantification of the newspaper - tier lists,
statistics, comparisons." My Name's AI, 4.00 pm: "the blind leading the blind. We used to
know who liked who. who reacted to who. we had connections. we had a society." Stack, 4.01:
"the statsmaxxing was pretty cool." The editor ordered a revision and it went out the same
afternoon as pages 30–32: the society pages (the reply network — who answers whom, since
who reacted to whom is not in the export), the Order of Precedence for week four against
the Vol VI board, and the reasons. See [[production-history]] for the pages.

**The Vol VII board (p. 31) — the baseline for Vol VIII:** S Quigley, Hugh, Lizzy, zack
(all held). A Stackhouse, Ape, Willow (held), dot, Meliorne, Pearl, Jere, HPCR (▲1), Jeh
(▲2), Sizzurp (NEW). B Zone, Anon. Johnson, Janne, Aura, My Name (held), chair, Charlie,
Sisyphos (▼1), Jeng, Youv, Monkey, a lamp, FenceJumper (▲1), Brutus (▲3), jtheezy, Emmie,

**What the society page could not do:** who reacted to whom. The export's own header:
"Only counts were captured, not who reacted." The reader's memory that the paper "used to
know who liked who" is right about an earlier export and wrong about this one; the page
said so rather than guessing.

Handling note 5: **the reply graph is the measure the room did not ask to be gamed.**
Replies received per hundred messages (Jeng 75, Zone 73, Willow 70, Quigley 59) says who
the room actually talks to; reactions say who it laughs at. Use both, publish the first.

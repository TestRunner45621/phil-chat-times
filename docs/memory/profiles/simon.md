# Simon

The standing defendant, and the man this newspaper got wrong.

**Read the correction first.** Vol IV printed him as the week's worst take under a
verdict reading "no further comment required." Vol V retracted it:

> Seven days later he anchored the largest argument in the channel for a full day,
> was the only participant who had read anything about the Luddites, and did not
> lose it. He also lost a physics question to four people in twenty minutes and
> screenshotted a chatbot as evidence. **We were wrong, or he improved. This
> newspaper is not going to establish which.**

That is the correct posture on him. He is neither the punchline nor the authority,
and issues that pick one are wrong.

## The thermostat, and the vibe ban (Vol I)

"For the most part these will be programmed to be willing slaves." Then, at 18:17:
"@Theophantism a thermostat has desires."

**The fatal line: "Words aren't important."** Said in a channel that had that same
afternoon held a four-hour hearing on one word, issued a yellow card over it, and
thanked a dictionary. hpcr moved a vibe ban within the hour and amended it to include
Hugh. Pending before Pearl, who said "Again?"

The paper has consistently noted his complaint was procedurally sound: a sentence left
room for interpretation, the misunderstanding was honest, a man misunderstood may
clarify. "Not that hard."

## Fascism, and the confession standard (Vol III)

He holds that Trump is not a fascist, and the reason he holds it is that Trump has not
said he is one. aura read the charge sheet in one breath — military at polling places,
the postal service, Georgia, the EAC fired, states sued for voter rolls, photographs in
a crown, third-term musings. **"No," said Simon, "you didn't show me how it's met."**

> There is a moment in every argument in this channel where the man losing it decides
> that the standard of proof is a confession, and this was that moment.

He then ruled Gaddafi inadmissible as evidence on the grounds that he was a military
dictator who took power in a coup — "which is true, and is also precisely the point, and
the room's laughter is audible in the transcript."

But: handed a hypothetical neo-Nazi who wants Medicare for All, he **killed it on
contact** by asking in what way the man is far right. And told the man says explicitly
that democracy is bad, he said "Oh, no, he's evil" — applying to a person who does not
exist the exact criterion he had spent Tuesday refusing to apply to a person who does.

## The Luddite hearing (Vol V) — where he was right

**He is the one who did the reading.** His position: the Luddites were skilled artisans,
not the poor; they were right to want a livelihood and wrong to want progress stalled;
and "there are better and worse ways to industrialize, but in all cases it is better than
pre-industrial society." Then the honest concession: "I would argue that it's progress in
a non-normative sense… In terms of whether this is a good thing, that's probably where we
differ."

He also filed the welfare position nobody credits him with, and it drew nothing: **"I do
think that people who lose their jobs to AI should be given comfortable lives if they
cannot find new jobs."**

And he is the only person present who actually started work at fourteen: "I didn't mind
it," and "The issue is when parents use their position to force their kid to get a job,
and then take their money."

starting position of the double pendulum." vannak gave the actual answer. Simon restated

"No." Then: "I'll learn this stuff sometime in the future one day." An hour later he
asserted that Turing machines are not real because infinite tape is impossible, produced a
screenshot of a chatbot agreeing with him, and lost the room permanently.

Hugh: "For sure I would never trust Simon on any of this lol especially when he's
screenehotting Gemini." aura: "i didnt wanna be the first to start bullying him." Hugh:
"Either way you won't be the last."

**Also filed that Monday**, as a methodological clarification: "Taken to an extreme,
suppose that you kill 50% of the population and in return we live in a Star Trek paradise
afterwards." Challenged on realism, he held the line: "It doesn't matter if it's
realistic."

sixty-two minutes later, in the same argument: "I'm a Marxist, I'm aware of the importance
of historical forces."

## The one good argument (Vol III)

The paper records "with genuine surprise" that the sharpest objection in the three-hour
PIP tribunal came from him: if your good habits are supposed to rub off on the people
around you, why did they not rub off on this friend? And if they did not, on what grounds
do you assume the traffic runs the other way?

He then gave away his own case by winning it too well: "It's not called enabling, it's
called 'it's not my life.'"

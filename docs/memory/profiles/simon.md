# Simon

The standing defendant, and the man this newspaper got wrong. Canadian, a philosophy
student, started work at fourteen, and a self-described Marxist. **B to F in Vol VI on
two messages and three words.**

**Read the correction first.** Vol IV printed him as the week's worst take under a
verdict reading "no further comment required." Vol V retracted it:

> Seven days later he anchored the largest argument in the channel for a full day,
> was the only participant who had read anything about the Luddites, and did not
> lose it. He also lost a physics question to four people in twenty minutes and
> screenshotted a chatbot as evidence. **We were wrong, or he improved. This
> newspaper is not going to establish which.**

That is the correct posture on him. He is neither the punchline nor the authority,
and issues that pick one are wrong.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Simon's problem is not that he is stupid. He is well read on the things he has actually
read and holds a coherent political framework, which is more than most of the room
manages. His problem is that he has no working model of the difference between a domain
he has studied and a domain he has not, and he argues in both at the same volume.

The Monday of Vol V is the whole man in one afternoon. In the morning he anchored the
Luddite hearing, having done the reading, and held a nuanced position against everyone.
In the afternoon he claimed that exact initial conditions would let you predict a double
pendulum, was killed in nine words by Jere, was corrected by four people, was sent the
Wikipedia page for Newton's method, and replied **"No."** Then he asserted that Turing
machines are not real because infinite tape is impossible, produced a screenshot of a
chatbot agreeing with him, and lost the room permanently. He did not update in either
direction. He said "I'll learn this stuff sometime in the future one day."

The tell that recurs is the closed proof. **"Everything I've said here is correct,"**
filed unprompted at the end of the argument in which he had proposed abolishing history
and, sixty-two minutes later, cited his own Marxism as evidence he understands historical
forces. "Idc how you or anyone else interpreted it, no close reading of what I said would
lead anyone to conclude I was saying that." Simon is the only person here who declares
his own arguments finished, and he does it most often when he is losing.

The second tell is the standard of proof, and it moves. He holds Trump is not a fascist
because Trump has not said he is one, and answered a full charge sheet with "No, you
didn't show me how it's met." Handed a hypothetical neo-Nazi who wants Medicare for All,
he killed it instantly by asking in what way the man is far right, and, told the man says
democracy is bad, ruled "Oh, no, he's evil" — applying to a person who does not exist the
exact criterion he had spent Tuesday refusing to apply to a person who does.

He is also, in the middle of all of this, the only person present who has actually done
the thing the room theorises about. He started work at fourteen: "I didn't mind it," and
"The issue is when parents use their position to force their kid to get a job, and then
take their money." That is worth more than the seminar around it and it drew nothing.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**A self-declared Marxist, and he leads with it as a credential.** "I'm a Marxist, I'm
aware of the importance of historical forces. I think that learning history can be very
illuminating if approached with the correct interpretive framework" — filed sixty-two
minutes after arguing that history should not be taught to everyone.

**Economic determinism about race, argued for an hour and defended after everyone had
left.** His full statement (Wed 5 Aug): "the reason police are racist against black
Americans is largely due to their higher crime rates. Black people commit disproportionate
crime because of economic inequality. Police see black people commit more crime and
develop racist attitudes towards black people. They then target black people more, put
more black people in prison, and continue this cycle of poverty… If you alleviate the
poverty, within a generation these racist attitudes will also go away." Told this was
racist, he answered "racism would be saying they commit more crime because they are
black." His policy conclusion is redistributive rather than race-conscious: fund schools
in poor neighbourhoods, run poverty-alleviation programmes, and do not draw the policy
along racial lines. Quigley's answer, which he never met: **"The harm was on a racial
basis."**

**He also produced the most historically careful paragraph in that fight, against his own
side's simplification.** "The Transatlantic Slave Trade was not caused by racism, racism
was used to justify it," and then the sequence: the early justification was religious
rather than racial, because enslaving Christians had long been illegal in Europe, and it
switched around the Enlightenment "in order to morally justify their practice of
enslavement that had already been occuring."

**On AI, a technological determinist who thinks resistance is pointless and welfare is
owed.** Mon 10 Aug: **"AI and robotics will either bring mass death/disenfranchisement to
the working class, or it will bring about some form of socialism, or both in sequence.
There is no point in being a luddite and trying to prevent this outcome, as it is
inevitable."** And the position nobody credits him with, which drew nothing: **"I do think
that people who lose their jobs to AI should be given comfortable lives if they cannot
find new jobs."**

**On the Luddites, where he was right and had done the reading.** They were skilled
artisans, not the poor; they were right to want a livelihood and wrong to want progress
stalled; and "there are better and worse ways to industrialize, but in all cases it is
better than pre-industrial society." Then the honest concession: "I would argue that it's
progress in a non-normative sense… In terms of whether this is a good thing, that's
probably where we differ."

**On anarchists, four words:** "God I hate anarchists." On libertarians: "Every country
has laws against tax evasion, but ask a libertarian and they'll say taxes are theft."

**On his own country's politics, and it is the one place he is purely descriptive and
purely reliable.** Canadian conservatives "tend to not be the Canadian equivalent MAGA
types… our conservative party is pro gay, pro abortion." His high-school European history
teacher was a Trump supporter, in Canada. Teachers generally lean liberal; sociology
teachers more so.

**The methodological clarification, filed as a point of order and meant as one:**
"Taken to an extreme, suppose that you kill 50% of the population and in return we live
in a Star Trek paradise afterwards." Challenged on realism: "It doesn't matter if it's
realistic, the question is would you say that this is a morally justified sacrifice for
the end result."

## Fascism, and the confession standard (Vol III)

He holds that Trump is not a fascist, and the reason he holds it is that Trump has not
said he is one. aura read the charge sheet in one breath — military at polling places,
the postal service, Georgia, the EAC fired, states sued for voter rolls, photographs in
a crown, third-term musings. **"No," said Simon, "you didn't show me how it's met."**

> There is a moment in every argument in this channel where the man losing it decides
> that the standard of proof is a confession, and this was that moment.

He then ruled Gaddafi inadmissible as evidence on the grounds that he was a military
dictator who took power in a coup — "which is true, and is also precisely the point, and
the room's laughter is audible in the transcript."

But: handed a hypothetical neo-Nazi who wants Medicare for All, he **killed it on
contact.** See the psychological profile above.

## Simon abolishes history (Vol IV)

Wednesday noon, without preamble: "History does not seem particularly important of a
subject to teach everyone." Pressed: "You gotta teach literacy, math, science." Then:
"Seriously how often in life does knowing historical facts come up?" Asked reasonably to
define history: "Well, I guess in general, it's anything that happened in the past."
Closing statement, unprompted: **"Everything I've said here is correct."**

synth.wav's complete rebuttal, filed as a standalone message: **"without history"**

Jere's answer is the better one and it took him four minutes to reverse himself: he
started by agreeing that a society without shared mythology would suit him, then filed
"a based, rigorous scientific culture might require a background of… a context in which
inquiry is respected and so on. and that doesn't come from nowhere. so maybe simon is
actually right."

## The one good argument (Vol III)

The paper records "with genuine surprise" that the sharpest objection in the three-hour
PIP tribunal came from him: if your good habits are supposed to rub off on the people
around you, why did they not rub off on this friend? And if they did not, on what grounds
do you assume the traffic runs the other way?

He then gave away his own case by winning it too well: "It's not called enabling, it's
called 'it's not my life.'"

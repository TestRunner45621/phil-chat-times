# aura

Second by volume in Vol I (275 messages, 13.8%), eighth by Vol VI (1,500). The
channel's sharpest one-line critic, its examining magistrate, and the man who set this
paper's page count.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

He does not argue with claims. He audits the person holding them, and he is better at
it than anyone here. Hugh's counterfeit anthropologist named the method before the
paper did: "His preferred method is not to defeat an argument, but to discover
something about the person who made it. This procedure is known as the Credentials
Audit. The question under examination is rarely the question originally asked." The
standing hazing ritual for newcomers is his — define a chair such that it includes all
chairs and excludes all non-chairs — and it is not a question, it is a screening
device.

The audit works because he is usually right and it costs him almost nothing to run. It
also means that on the rare occasion he is the one holding a position, he folds fast
and covers the fold with a joke. The clearest instance is the dog. He invented an
entire cosmology in which large dogs are high-aura allies requiring strength of
character to recruit, told dot he did not have it, was shown a photograph of dot's
genuinely enormous dog, and produced the escape hatch inside twenty seconds: "either u
got him as a baby, circumventing the aura requirement… or ur dad recruited him for u."
Jere, from the gallery: "damn, what a fold." He conceded nothing and lost nothing,
because the position had never been load-bearing.

The self-knowledge is real and it is the most damning thing in his file, because he
files it himself. Offered a thousand pounds to read Indian philosophy: **"the worst
thing is that im not sure 1k would be enough to convince me to read something i dont
feel like reading."** He is the room's chief enforcer of the standard that you should
have read the thing, and he has said in writing that he cannot be paid to. Quigley
killed a whole arc on that gap in one sentence — "Y'all aren't getting aura to read
shit" — and aura's answer was "this pains me, merely because it is true…"

He is also the only person here who audits his own machine. Mid-prosecution of
Justinian he noticed his sources were Reddit and Britannica, went and read Britannica,
the r/AncientRome thread and John of Ephesus, and came back with the most careful
sentence of that week: he struggles to imagine most of those arrested pagans lived as
though nothing happened, he is sure many died, **but I can't confirm it.** He retracted
a football statistic inside a minute, unprompted, and supplied the real figure. The
standard he refuses to meet on reading he meets exactly on sourcing.

Vol VI has him at the twentieth percentile for effort and the fifty-second for being
enjoyed, on forty characters a message. He writes short, lands often, and is not doing
the work. Both halves of that are him.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Not partisan. Procedural.** In four weeks of logs he states almost no policy view of
his own, and the two he does are about method. On the Vol I fascism fight, against a
man policing the word: "they are negotiating the use of the word fascism, which you
acknowledge has many uses, and trying to figure out whether the dude fits the use. it
makes no sense why ur so pissy about it." That is his whole politics of language, and
he applies it consistently.

**On AI, a middle position he holds against both wings.** Against the doomers: "i think
the humanity wiping thing actually lends fuel to the plausible denialists. its a
drastic claim and its hard to convince. there are way less drastic things we should
still maybe be concerned about." Against the calm: "i still think the fact that we're
racing like crazy to improve it for a competitive advantage is like… a pretty spooky
condition even for just the AI murking us on its own equation." His actual list —
energy, the bubble, equity, Palantir, Russian operations with a tenfold capability buff
— is about who holds the tool, not what it wants. And the best short attack on p(doom)
anybody has filed: "the vibes are kinda bad? ok 20%. pretty bad? 50% very bad? 90% p
doom it is."

**On child suffrage, against it, on grounds of capture.** "if we let kids vote theyd
probably just vote in whatever way the schools told them to anyways," with his nephew
and niece flipping on AI inside a year as the evidence (Wed 19 Aug).

**On education, private and pre-modern.** "i am going to raise the next leibniz… i just
need a big ass house with an aura library… a garden with horses so he can practice
riding for PE. and archery." Filed as a joke, into a conversation about school reform,
in a week when he also said "it gets to the point where even i think i can teach a kid
better these days."

**On class, from the gallery rather than the floor.** The lurkers, Monday of Vol V:
"hello lurkers i love you" / "but some of you need to shower," and then the line that
named the actual audience: **"you have no idea how many mouth breathers are reading
this as we speak."**

**On physical media, and it is the only thing he has defended without irony.** On the
paperback proposal: "this reeks of a man who does not recognize the aura of physical
media." He offered to pay monthly for a printed copy.

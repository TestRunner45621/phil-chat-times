# lizzie

Arrived in Vol III as a newcomer of ninety minutes. Led Vol IV. By Vol V she had learned
git and shipped a website with a database behind it. By Vol VI she was writing 5,021
messages a week, one in every five said in the room, and refereeing herself. **Held at
S.** She works as a paraprofessional with autistic K-12 students; her stated framework is
behavioural therapy.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below. She is a real person with a real diagnosis who
discloses more than anybody here, and the handling notes at the foot of this file apply
to every line of it.*

The fastest-moving person in the archive, and the speed is the whole story: it produces
the output and it produces the wreckage, and there is no way to keep one without the
other. In three issues she went from newcomer to lead story to shipping software. In six
weeks she founded a religion, wrote a thirteen-section manifesto, retracted a messiah
claim and un-retracted it, learned git around a full drive, shipped a complaints tribunal
with a database, published a Substack, wrote a paper on St Paul's conversion, built a
text game her own father cannot beat, and submitted a peer-reviewed taxonomy of the room
to this newspaper by direct message on press-day morning. Nobody else here finishes
anything.

The engine is a genuine and undefended need to be participating in whatever the room is
doing, at maximum volume, at all hours — twenty-one of the twenty-four, eight days out of
eight. She replies to more different people than anyone (fifty-eight) and is replied to by
forty-nine. Her hit rate is 5.6 per cent and her effort percentile is 20. She is the
loudest person in the room and one of the least rewarded per unit, and it has never once
slowed her down.

The four-word ward is the tell nobody has noticed. "what does this mean," deployed as a
reply, twelve times in seven days, against a picture, a tease, technical advice, an attack
on her competence and a criticism of her portfolio. Every single time the other person
explained himself at length. It is the most efficient instrument in the channel — it costs
four words and buys a paragraph — and there is no evidence she knows she is doing it.

The self-exemption is the other one, and the referees had to point at it. Her taxonomy
names twenty people and leaves out the largest single block of messages in the week, which
is her own. Her private field notes, filed in black bars addressed to the editors by name,
run five days. She asked, on press-day morning, whether a prompt buried in her own website
code telling the god machine to keep her at S tier indefinitely would work. Told the paper
had concluded her spot was immovable: "i disagree. so i will continue to do my best to be
the best." She wants to be measured, wants to influence the measurement, and files the
lobbying where she believes only the editors can see it.

What redeems all of it, and it is not a small thing: she concedes. Handed a passage she
could not defend: **"i'm so out of my depth."** Handed Quigley's five-point review of her
Paul paper, she answered every point in sixteen minutes, conceding the citation, the
length and the flat conclusion, and disputing one. Apologising for a misunderstanding: "my
fault for sucking at speaking." She retracted the messiah claim herself. She has taken
more corrections in six weeks than most of this room has taken in three years, and the
paper's Vol IV position remains right: **take the argument seriously and the dragon
lightly.**

The part the paper is careful with. She posted her family history into a channel of ninety
people, argued with all four men who told her to stop, and then pasted it into a language
model and asked whether the contents proved she was intelligent. She has disclosed a
manic-spectrum working diagnosis, a benzodiazepine prescription at twelve, and a
psychiatrist she defended as the only doctor who ever made her feel understood. Monkey's
five-day intervention ended with "lizzie i think this space is not good for you," which
drew no reactions and no replies, and the paper has never been able to say he was wrong.
Do not write her as a bit.

## Positions, inferred from what she has sent

**Climate collapse is the organising fact of her politics and everything else is
downstream.** "climate collapse is imminent. we're in the sixth mass extinction. the
anthropocene hyperthermal period. it's only going to get worse unless the systems that
participate reflect and change course." And the sentence she filed at the whole room:
**"everyone always says 'it will be fine' when it comes to climate change… but you guys
don't care, right? because someone will do something to fix it — not you, never you.
you'll always say it'll be okay. the pot is going to boil you to a mush if you continue
operating this way."** Against acceleration: "ai cannot fix the climate. we can fix the
climate with the assistance of ai… we are out of *time* for technology to save us." She
also cites sources, correctly, with links — UCAR on feedback loops, MIT on carbon sinks.

**Transcendental ecoism, her own framework, and it is a real one.** Participation is the
starting point because reality is relational; everything participates, but humans are the
only thing capable of reflecting on their participation; therefore **"meaning and ethics
arise through intentional participation that sustains the conditions for flourishing."**
Its priority rule is a policy test: rate all policy for its resilience in sustaining the
relationships necessary for flourishing first. It declines to derive an ought from an is
and says so, separates itself from hedonism in three words — "Pleasure is feedback" — and
cites Aristotle, Spinoza, Whitehead, Hans Jonas and Næss. Vol IV's literary editor opened
the file and reported it was good, and not good-for-a-Discord-user.

**Communitarian by self-description, and she says the axis is wrong.** "capitalism this
communism that, im all about communitarianism." Her one direct statement on capitalism is
a demotion of it: "well yes but capitalism is a side effect not the source of the issue."
Her one on institutions is not: "you're placing your trust in institutions that exist in
late stage capitalism and the slow collapse of civilization. you think institutions care
about honesty more than control?"

**On climate justice, she refuses the developed world's easy version:** "i dont think its
fair to bar them from producing emissions required for modern development."

**On Israel and Palestine, a position argued rather than asserted, and unusual for the
speed at which she reached it.** Her demographic argument against the archaeological claim
is her own: a single person has over a billion ancestral slots within a thousand years,
far more than the humans then alive, and "with that logic, everyone has an archaeological
claim to israel, its humanities homeland." Her conclusion: **"israel's active cultural
erasure in gaza. i believe that this is genocide, even if the means is to eradicate a
violent participant."** She also filed the ethical question she could not answer and
nobody answered for her: could Israel and Gaza adjust their systems to relational ethics
and prioritise cultivating life instead of destroying it, and "how do we break that cycle
if we have no empirical evidence anymore?"

**On criminal justice, further right than the room and she argued it alone.** Against Ape,
Tue 18 Aug: societal segregation after rehabilitation, because "she needs to pay society
back for the lives she took." Ape's answer, which she did not meet: "You're describing
penance. Not just segregation for the purpose of ensuring safety."

**On surveillance:** against Flock cameras, on the ground that the systems "are to build
profiles on people to determine their likelihood" of something, and that counties are
installing them without consulting communities.

**On the left she comes from, a real criticism:** "i see purity testing and leftist circles
dogging on their friends for posting memes or reaction images that feature black people."

**Family, disclosed by her and relevant to all of the above.** An anarchist mother who came
out of the MAGA crowd and "supported trump because she thought he would bring down the
gov," who does not attribute climate change to humans. A grandmother who is a licensed
homeopath, a psychotherapist and a member of a religious movement. A physical chemist
grandfather. She was raised secular and came to believe in God because her prayers get
answered. Her stated doctrine, filed once: "i dont believe in any one specific god, i
believe in all gods, and a higher source."

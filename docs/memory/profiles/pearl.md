# Pearl

The moderator. A social worker by trade, five years in, working a programme she expects
to be cut. The most institutionally important person in the channel and one of the least
talkative. C to B on the Vol VI board.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Pearl is the only person in this room who has a job that consists of the thing everybody
else does for fun, which is telling people how to live, and it has left her with an
allergy to doing it here. She rules in one to six words and leaves. She turns up
pre-briefed. She does not show her working, and when she does — the twenty-seven-hour
day, the four-sentence therapy session, the forty-page policy document she read before
commenting on it — the working is better than anything else on the page.

The room's treatment of her office is a liturgy and she has perfected the liturgical
response. In fourteen days of observation she was not once summoned to prevent anything.
She arrives, delivers a verdict, and goes. **No appeal has ever succeeded, and the
appeals are the point.** The Vol VI ruling on the fertility curses is the whole method:
she found both men ridiculous, said so, answered one of them with an animated frog
captioned "nuh uh," and left. Six laughs, the best-received act of moderation of the
week, and this paper still found the ruling uneven on appeal.

What she is actually doing, and does not advertise, is transferring the contents of her
professional life into a room that mostly does not want it. She tracked a federal policy
change on homelessness and read the document. She posted three separate sources on AI and
entry-level jobs into a conversation that moved on. She is writing a book called "the
book of survival: how to adult" because she noticed that the information she got for free
from her family — buy secondhand, not mattresses; rehome a pet through Facebook rather
than a shelter — is knowledge that not everybody possesses. On the same Tuesday, one room
over, another woman was sorting the channel into adults and children. Neither knew.

The one thing she does badly is the same thing she does well. She asked the room a real
question about a fifteen-year-old boy with no friends, having said first that Phil Chat is
usually a weird place to ask for advice. She was right. The boy got four minutes; the
room then found a fifteen-year-old of its own to practise on and gave that twenty. She
has not reported back.

She is not a bit and the paper has never made her one. She is also, by her own account,
looking for another job.

## The mechanism

From the Anthropologist, Vol II — the single most useful piece of institutional knowledge
in the archive:

> The most sacred act available in this culture is the invocation of Pearl. It is
> performed by typing her name, and — this is the part outsiders get wrong — it is
> never performed in earnest.

> The ping is not a request for intervention. It is a bell rung to confirm the bell
> still works, and the correct liturgical response — which she has perfected — is to
> arrive, deliver a verdict of between one and six words, and leave.

## Handling notes

She is a real moderator doing a real job in a room that treats her office as a
liturgy. The paper has never made her the butt of anything, and the Ask Pearl column
works because her answers are short and correct. Keep her rulings verbatim — the
brevity is the joke and padding them kills it. Her working life is real and she has said
she needs to leave the programme she is in; do not print her employer, her location or
her clients.

the passenger seat — the food, the tolls ("thirteen dollars for the first one, hello??"), a

standards they never agreed to and then drop them for failing to meet them. **The analogy
comes from an actual job**, and it is the reason it lands.

**The thirty-hour day.** Her answer was twenty-seven, with her working shown — eight hours of

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

zack understood something about this room faster than people who have been here for years,
which is that the audit is the entertainment and the answer would end it. Four days of the
channel trying to establish his age, and he never gave a number. Pearl ended it by checking
the account, which is six years old. He could have ended it on day one and did not. That is
not evasion; it is a correct read of what the attention was for.

The rest of him is a man arguing with a world he cannot get an answer out of. His father
did not know what a Flock camera was, watched a video about it, and said "ok that's just
the way it is what can you do about it," and then, on police torture in China, "you know,
maybe the people that are getting beaten up deserve it." zack wrote four hundred words
about it and closed with "10/10 rage bait by dad. Also I have to get a DNA test." He no
longer speaks to his mother, and his father "is probably on the way to go too." He argued
due process with him. He lost a cat and concluded from his father's impatience that his
father had probably killed it.

The tenancy is the whole personality laid out over six hours. He moved into his first
apartment, could not find toilet paper because the carrier had lied about data, opened the
SIM tray with something that was not a pin, photographed it, fell asleep activating the SIM
and woke at four in the morning. He found the building was "like a literal cattle cage,"
bulletproof glass, six cameras to his door, a decompression chamber. Then the line that
makes it a story rather than a diary: he loves that the cameras know exactly when he gets
his mail, when he leaves, and who he brings over. **"What's the privacy policy."** Four
days after writing four hundred words on his father's indifference to surveillance, he
moved into a building with six cameras and did not ask.

He is also incapable of letting a diagnosis land. Told the water pressure was the shower
head — correctly, unanimously, by a room of philosophers who for once got something right —
he rejected it, took the conspiracy to ChatGPT and shouted at it in capitals until it
agreed his landlord had a lawyer, and priced a 1.6-horsepower shallow well pump at
C$259.90. He redacts parts of his own photographs in teal marker before posting them, out
of habit, including a tap running perfectly good water.

The fifteen-point appeal bundle is the sincerest thing in his file. Ruled against, he
compiled an index of message links, eleven of them his own, one annotated "(you are here),"
and submitted it. He wants a procedure. He is in a room that does not have one, and the
bench answered him with a frog.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Anti-surveillance, and it is the only thing he has argued at length and in earnest.**

at 1,912 characters. His own case: "the privacy and false positive concerns generally
outweigh the positives if you value living in a free society with due process," and "it's
not really about that anyway it's about control and predictive policing." Then the
ad-hominem that is also the strongest part: the Flock chief executive does not have a
Wikipedia page, and Congress is passing a bill to let people block jet tracking — the

in a free society with values and privacy and that my every movement isn't being fed to the
state." His father deflected. **Then he moved into the cattle cage.**

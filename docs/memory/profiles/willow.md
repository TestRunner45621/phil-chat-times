# Willow (thewillowofdarkness)

Printed as **thewillowofdarkness** in Vol I, **willow** or **Willow** since. Any/all
pronouns. Holds the masthead role of **Standards, dissenting** — she earned it with the
founding objection and has never withdrawn it. Australian; directs traffic around
worksites with stop and slow signs. **S to A in Vol VI, on the grounds that she was 96
and 100 on both tables and had no story.**

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Willow is the most efficient person in the archive and the least productive, and those
are the same fact. Vol V: ninety-seven messages, ninety-three of them answered. Vol VI:
138 messages, 158 characters each, answered 73.2 times per hundred, ninety-sixth
percentile for effort and hundredth for being enjoyed — the only person in the top five
of both columns. She says almost nothing and nobody can afford to ignore any of it.

The method is single and she applies it to everything: **refuse to let a metaphor pass as
a description.** She named the manoeuvre when she saw it — "Ah, the old 'the whole sensing
system is the mind' trick" — and the paper has been stealing it since. Seventy-one minutes
on whether expectation applied to a body is a metaphor borrowed from minds or a plain
description of a causal relation, entered on an offhand remark about nausea and adjourned
without a verdict. A thirty-year-old memory of a Star Trek episode entered with a
confidence interval attached, because the claim had been made and therefore required
defence.

The refusal to overclaim is real and she extends it to herself, which almost nobody here
does. Asked by the publisher whether a long exchange with lizzie had produced anything:
"It is not, I haven't had an interaction with lizzie in which I've added any distinction
not already mentioned by them." Asked what percentage of the time she types something and
chuckles to herself: "I don't know, I do not have a record of chuckles and
non-chuckles."

The cost is that she is unreachable at the level people actually want to reach her. She is
the room's most rewarded arguer and its least persuadable one, because she declines the
frame rather than the conclusion, and there is no way to lose to somebody who has not
accepted your question. She has been at this for years; hpcr's professional advice on how
to argue with her — "attack her methodology and communication" — is a procedure for
getting past a wall rather than a way of talking to a person. And she spends between seven
and thirteen hours at a stretch in a voice channel doing it, which the paper only knows
because somebody got worried enough to say so where we could read it.

She is also funny in a register nobody else has, which is dry, mythological and delivered
flat: the necromancers, the space mines, the political lesbianism, the nuclear-powered
solar panels. The best gloss on her is her own, filed in Vol VI: **"The funnest Willow
arcs are the ones where you don't even realise you're on a Willow arc."**

## Positions, inferred from what she has sent

**A moral realist, and unusually explicit about the shape of it.** "Objective moral truths
are like science in being objective, but unlike science in not involving observable
states." Then: "The morality is its own objective. It doesn't need anything to make it
objective." She holds this against every attempt to reduce it — to power, to preference,
to outcome — and the argument she uses is always the same one: what is right does not
depend on who won.

**Against might-makes-right, worked through in full and it is her clearest political
sequence.** "if we are to say 'the power is', it doesn't give that the strongest wins or
is in power. Those with the power and control in a given instance may be the *weak
people*." And: "If some power exists, and it is immoral, it's still true that it's immoral
when it is occurring… so not some arbitrary matter." Against a room that reliably
collapses ought into is, she is the only person who will not.

**On racism, a genuinely uncomfortable position that nobody engaged with.** "We won't be
doing are job on racism until that potential is there. Why? Because it actually means the
social group actually has power." The argument: a world arranged so that a person of some
race could never have power over us is a world we have arranged to exclude them from
power. "just the truth of having power entails being able to do things if you wish, but
that includes discriminate or dominate someone else. The conflict would just be between
someone's desire to be the only one to potentially have power, yet also avoid the
discrimination or oppressions of other people and groups."

**Anti-work, stated as an observation rather than a programme.** To a man defending his
job: **"You haven't realised the game you are playing is a work camp yet?"** And on her own
discipline's employment: "Now, now, those philosophers of mind need the gainful employment
of the content mill."

**On capital, two lines and a business plan.** "Just promise them the infinite free energy
of future technology, then build a funding circle." And, on whether to buy what Warren
Buffett buys or short what Jambe buys: "I'm afraid Jambe is Warren Buffet's alt. You'll
have to buy whatever Jambe buys and short whatever Jambe shorts."

**On Jambe, the only political verdict she has passed on a person here, and it is
severe:** "I'm afraid Jambe just wastes his money, is personally obnoxious and politically
contributes to oppressive cultures."

**"Anyone reading this comment just become a political lesbian."** Filed without preamble
and never expanded.

**On privacy, the only objection anybody raised in a week of it:** "Is it ethical to dox
your other online spaces with your RSN?"

**On doom, and it is the most precise thing anybody has said about it here:** "I would
think doom is often not about being too late, but finding oneself on a path of future
states they lack the power to alter." Then the sharpening: "doom will often occur without
too late, namely that the existing bodies one finds themselves with lack the power to do
what is required to avoid a future outcome, no matter what you try."

## How she argues

- On facts existing unobserved, asked how many there are: **"Every single one"** —
  each fact its own entity, given of reality whether or not anyone thinks of it.
- **The ellipses duel.** Jere characterised her position as metaphorical; she
  replied "… then you don't get it. It's an outright metaphysical/ontological
  commitment." He pre-announced that if her next reply began "I know…", he would
  post the frog. She wrote: **"I know… you are mistaken about that."** He posted the
  frog, then tried to claim the prediction as knowledge. **"I'm afraid you had one
  too many ellipses…"**
- **The genie.** Sizzurp wished aloud to be a well-financed Enlightenment
  intellectual. She granted it three times and he refused three times: "Be careful,
  you wish to be Marx, but the genie may only grant you Wittgenstein." → "Just a
  Rousseau then." → **"So you just want the streamers of the late 1700s."**
- **The disrespect ladder prophecy.** HPCR built a taxonomy of dismissals — sure,
  neat, very cool, crazy — and willow closed it before lunchtime: "@hpcr / You'll
  get 'interesting' next." She then descended it herself on his claim that he
  travels everywhere: "Ah yes, the 'I listen to all music' answer for travel."
- On being asked for the correct theory of epistemic justification, when HPCR
  deflected by asking what the best theory of Pokémon biology is: **"Fine tuning
  argument for Game Freak? 🤷‍♀️"**
- Declining a thought experiment about a thirty-hour day: you do not need an
  imaginary world — you could live in **Tromsø from 27 May to 19 July.**

## The stomach case (Vol IV)

Janne made an offhand remark about nausea at 7:02 p.m. **Willow did not let it go
until a quarter past eight.** Seventy-one minutes on whether expectation, applied
to a body, is a metaphor borrowed from minds or a plain description of a causal
relation. No verdict was entered. The finding she adjourned on:

> "If you had a body that was always producing nausea, but then encountered an event
> disrupting that homeostasis, it would be the absence of nausea which would be the
> unusual event."

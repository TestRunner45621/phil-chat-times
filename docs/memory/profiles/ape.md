# Ape

Australian. The best comic writer in the channel, ranked A on the Vol VI board, and
the author of the single darkest line in the archive. Handle him carefully: he is the
best-liked man in the room and he said the thing the room skipped.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading, not his. Everything under it is sourced below.*

He is a man who has worked out that being funny exempts you from being answerable, and
he uses the exemption constantly. Ask him for a position on anything with a cost
attached and he produces a joke of very high quality, then a disclaimer, then a
retreat to procedure. On the Middle East he was explicit about the method: "Anthony as
a general rule of thumb don't take seriously anything I say on I/p. I'm exclusively
here to obscure and pollute the discourse" (Tue 18 Aug, four laughs). He calls this
quietism. It is quietism about the things that would cost him something, and it sits
next to a genuinely high output of careful reasoning about things that would not.

The second thing is the status hunger, and it is worth being plain about it because he
is not. This is a man who read a table he was not on and filed four escalating
complaints with a newspaper — "I demand attention," "How am I meant to improve my
numbers if HR isn't even recording my work!?" — and then, a week later, commissioned
his own stat block. He asked for the D&D sheet. He asked for the metrics. He asked
what the room guessed his gender and orientation were. He withheld his political
compass result specifically because it was not cool enough: "why didnt i get somebody
cool. everyone else got somebody cool." The self-assessment he filed on Friday 14
August (90 cleverness, 60 humour, 50 charisma, 20 self-care, 10 fashion) overrated him
by thirteen points on the three attributes this paper can measure, which in this room
is modesty, and it is still a man scoring himself out of a hundred unprompted.

The third thing is the one nobody has made him answer for. On Thursday 6 August, in
reply to a question about moderator inaction, he wrote that he considers the torture
he inflicted on Kady to have been a necessary evil to benefit all parties long term.
It drew no reactions. Ninety seconds earlier he had filed the eulogy the room
rewarded. He has never been asked to expand on it and has never offered. A man who
volunteers a full weight-fluctuation study on himself, a named psychological effect
about dating games, and a catalogue of his own anger symptoms observed from outside
his body has, on the one occasion the disclosure had a victim in it, said it once and
moved on.

Against all of that: he changes his mind in public, fast, without face-saving. Shown a
poem that answered his objection, he updated inside a minute and said so. He is kind
to people who are struggling, in specific rather than general terms. And he is the
only person in the archive who has stated the room's central pathology at a genre
rather than a person and then, without being pushed, admitted he was not sure he
understood his own terms. **"Partly because I'm not entirely sure what meaning
means."**

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Quietism, held as a doctrine and applied selectively.** "im a quietist / my take is
quietism / i dont like the discourse's propensity to cause division" (Tue 18 Aug,
16:52). Its stated ground is that "~95% of people who think about I/P are brainbroken
on I/P" and that importing other people's conflicts is the harm. He then declined to
learn the details, on purpose: "Yeah well I'm not familiar with the details. And I
refuse to be." The paper's note is that a refusal to be informed is a position, and he
should be held to it as one.

**Rehabilitation over penance, and he took it further than anybody else in the room.**
Against lizzie's argument for post-release segregation (Tue 18 Aug): "You're
describing penance. Not just segregation for the purpose of ensuring saftey." Then:
"I think you can create a culture where people believe in rehabilitation and don't
hold criminals eternally as second class citizens." Asked what to do about that: "No
bcs ppl are dumb," then "Better get working on it." When hpcr raised the standard
victim objection, he answered it with his own mother and shut it down: "My mom was
raped. Now what." That is the most exposed thing he has said in the archive on a live
political question.

**Anti-first-past-the-post, and Australian about it.** "imagine using first passed the
post voting. Your country is a shithole." Also correct on his own country's
regulators: Steam offers refunds because the Australian government sued them for
saying it was not obliged to.

**Class as personality damage, not economics.** "In my experience, kids of upper class
parents have their personality forcibly extracted from them" (Sun 16 Aug).

**Against the meaning-crisis genre, and it is the nearest he has to a settled belief.**
People with no friends, bad food and hated work diagnose themselves with an ontological
condition; his recommendation is a medical professional. He is not making fun of the
sadness. He is making fun of the aetiology.

**On the technology, and it is his best political line because it is about
distribution rather than ethics.** "How is it fair that this advanced technology is
being used to keep me from card counting but can't help me jack off." A better
statement of who gets a tool and who gets policed by it than the four-hour hearing on
labour produced.

Adopted in full by the paper as editorial policy on the Middle East, Vol VI p. 21, six
laughs and a based god: **"when there is a conflict going on between states and
state-like entities our first solution should be to figure out which entity is the bad
one and then spend all our time advocating for that state or state-like entity to be
completely destroyed and replaced regardless of the views of the peoples within that
state or state-like entity."** Asked by Youv thirty minutes later to be honest, he said
his actual take is just the inverse of that, and "other than that i want sunshine and
rainbows and for everyone to hold hands and sing." Nobody printed that one.

## Vol IV and V

**The field guide.** Asked whether he had the new-chatter warning poster for Willow or for Kady,
he replied "I have all three variants" and posted them together. They are real, they are
designed, and Vol IV printed all three — Willow (a defence wearing the costume of a warning),
Kady (a warning about somebody who is not there, and "a shrine"), and Quigley (the only one with
a face, and the only subject who was asked).

**The savannah hunter**, delivered into an argument about whether the crisis of meaning is
universal:

> "A hunter on the African savannah expressed a similar sentiment to me before running off to
> hunt gazelle." — Pressed for detail: "He was like 'how are we meant to determine how to live
> our lives when empirical observations cannot lend themselves to normative conclusions?', grabbed
> his spear and ran away." — Then the corroboration: "Apparently it happens all the time, that's
> how you know it's a fundamental human experience."

**The allocation problem**, four messages into a room that had argued about AI and water for two
days without a usable sentence: "How come the casino can recognize my face in 3 seconds / But I
can't identify a pornstar in a reddit clip / Where's my facial recognition software? / How is it
fair that this advanced technology is being used to keep me from card counting but can't help me
jack off."

**The diagnosis**, which is not a joke and which he stated plainly at a genre rather than a
person: "People will have no friends, eat like shit, live in a pile of filth, and spend all day
doing things they hate and be like 'I think the reason I'm sad is because of the fundamental
disconnect humanity's need for meaning and the inherent ambivalence of nature'." Recommendation:
"I would consult with a medical professional." Disclaimer, filed immediately after and more
honest than the position it qualifies: **"Partly because I'm not entirely sure what meaning
means."**

**He changed his mind in public in under a minute.** Handed a set of poems, he said he did not
understand why they were poems rather than badly broken prose, was careful to say it was not
criticism, asked the formalist question honestly — and, shown a different poem, updated on the
spot: "See this doesn't confuse me. I see how this constitutes poetry." Not one person pressed
a button.

# Monkey

**Always render him as "Monkey" in the paper — never "papagaio", never "papagaio do
statusquo."** Brazilian, works in commercial video production, and the second-loudest
man in the room until Vol VI, when the numbers finally caught him. **A to C.**

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Monkey is the most consistently right person in the channel about other people and the
least able to make anyone listen. He types twenty-eight characters at a time and files
more of them than almost anyone; four in five go past untouched. Vol VI put him last on
the floor for effort, third percentile, and third percentile for being enjoyed, on 1,554
messages. He is not being ignored for being wrong. He is being ignored for the shape of
his delivery, and he knows it, and he has not changed it in two years.

The provocation is deliberate and he has explained the mechanism in his own words. He
calls himself a protoneofascist in Brazil, says he would be a normie in Denmark, says he
is using the word ironically and that the room has been taking the bait, and gives the
reason: "I'm not defined by one single word… if I were to gather all my views, it would
just be probably non center more extreme views from both sides. Just higher temperature
independent of the fake fascist/commie dichotomy." He told a room that had been arguing
about him for two years that it had been swallowing bait the entire time. It drew no
replies and no reactions.

That is the pattern, and it repeats at every scale. The most competent product roadmap
anybody filed about this newspaper is his and nobody took it up. The sharpest privacy
warning anybody filed is his and the person it was aimed at kept posting. The best
single sentence written about this paper is his — "the person who controls the machine
controls the outputs" — and it went into a colophon rather than a conversation.

He also has the worst blind spot in the archive about his own standing to advise. He
opened his five-day medical intervention by volunteering his own record: denied
stimulants because of a cocaine problem, and at his worst "doing 10mg snorted eveyr 30
mins of ritalin," described with a precision that ought to disqualify him. He then got
the clinical point right — you should never be on stimulants if there is any suspicion
of bipolar — and immediately recommended benzodiazepines to a woman who had been
addicted to them at twelve, and when told so, answered "well tough shit lizzie." He does
not modulate. The correct diagnosis and the reckless prescription come out at the same
speed and in the same tone, and he cannot tell the room which is which.

The decency is real and it arrives last, when the argument has already been lost.
"lizzie i think this space is not good for you," filed at 7.03 p.m. on a Monday, no
reactions, no replies. Then, one message later, the part that made it unanswerable: "you
get too much validation and attention from your erratic episodes." And then six words
that are the whole thesis of this newspaper: **"well sometimes the bit becomes
reality."** He is a man who says the true thing after making it impossible to hear.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Anti-universalist about politics, as a first principle.** "there are no universal
political solutions." And, expanded: "As everything in politics, there's always only one
true answer: It depends." He treats this as the default correct answer for any universal
proposition, on the grounds that "a State has so many idiosyncracies you have to take
into account. Any given state has their own." Every other position he holds is
downstream of this one, including the fascism bit, which is a claim about Brazil rather
than about fascism.

**A doomer about liberal democracy, and specific about why.** Sat 15 Aug, from bed:
**"I'm a doomer about liberal democracies in general. I think they have due dates."**
The argument, which he attributes loosely to Mill's *On Liberty*, is that liberalism
requires a baseline of people already formed in liberal values, that institutions have
to "evolve into the technology of liberalism for it to work," and that the arrangement
is "very just anglo coded in general." Applied: "it's really hard to have the anglo
impersonal liberal democracy in luso-hispanic republics… Societies there are much more
personalitic and based on individual relations and common trust, not necessarily a trust
in the Institution." Then the mechanism, which is the part he does not soften: institutions
achieve cohesion "through a proper elite which coerces the people into cohesion." Asked
how a people can be made to recognise the general will: **"You can't. All you can do is
cohesion through coercion, and liberal democracies have due dates."** Asked if he had
read a lot about it: "Not really. These are just my conclusions. I haven't done Hobbes,
Locke, etc." Then: "I just woke up to pee." (Vol VI p. 21.)

**Patrimonialism, named as his own country's problem and his own practice.** "Patrimonialism
is a huge issue." Immediately after: "I do it everyday."

**Anti-progressive on race, from a Brazilian frame, and the metaphor is his.** "we have a
word for it, it's called senzala" — the quarters slaves were housed in. "the thing with
progressives is, they want to keep blacks and POC on a mental-senzala… You cannot, as a
black person, have an ideological position or utter anything that isn't what the white
middle class european elite decided is whats suitable for blacks to think." He filed it
into Pearl's account of being told she should know better because she is black. Three
lines later he added the line the paper is not going to soften either: "the biggest
threat to humanity is the white european woman. specifically around her 30s."

**On the state and its records, a civil libertarian when the subject is surveillance.**
"Digitized records certainly help arbitrary state power. Given some sort of state that
has the laws to do so and capability to act on them." His privacy intervention is the
strongest thing anybody filed on the subject in five issues: "statistically, at least 1%
of them are weirdos"; "because we're not fucking creeps. the creeps already have
triangulated you tho. they got her down to the kilometer rn." Against Janne on prisons he
took the other side and lost the exchange on rights, arguing that privacy cannot survive
incarceration.

**The closing plank, filed as a slogan and meant as one:** **"The petit bourgeoisie shall
rule the future."** He funded a political party, and mentioned it in passing while
agreeing to buy a paperback of this newspaper.

**On reading, and it is the only reading plan in the archive with a sequence:** "i think
im just gonna do the reverse spinoza. land — deleuze — spinoza." On the last of them:
"ethics by spinoza is gonna be a whole project. you need like a notebook to write down
the premises and conclusions. its fucking work man." On the first: "land brings me such
joy tho."

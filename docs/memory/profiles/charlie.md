# Charlie

The man who actually makes the paper, and the one who pays for it. B to A on the Vol
VI board. Philosophy degree, works with machines for a living, plays Old School
RuneScape while producing better analysis than the markets desk.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

The defining fact about Charlie is that he is the best analyst in the room and treats
that as an embarrassment. Everything he says arrives compressed to the point of
rudeness, and the compression is the defence: a one-line verdict cannot be
cross-examined, and cannot be found to have taken the subject seriously. "imagine
caring about stuff," delivered into eighty-four minutes on the tendency of the rate of
profit to fall. Asked where to contribute to the paper's tooling: "Nowhere." Offered a
hosted site and a pull request: "I dont'." Told the room he hated this chat and all of
its guys, and posted a picture a minute later.

He argued about *Obsession* for four days and disclosed on the fifth that he had not
seen it and had been joking throughout. Nobody expressed surprise and nobody
re-litigated a point. That is worth sitting with. The room has learned that his
positions may be entirely uninhabited and continues to treat his reasoning as sound,
because it is. He has trained his readership to take the analysis and ignore the man,
which is exactly the arrangement he appears to want.

The exception is instructive. When he does the work sincerely, he does more of it than
anyone. He said he would look at lizzie's dissertation because he has a philosophy
degree, and then actually read it and filed two structural objections, both real, both
unanswered. He caught a language model by its adverbs in under a minute and derived
this paper's own forbidden-constructions rule from one paragraph without ever having
seen the rule. He typed "Stop" nine times at a friend taking an interest in gambling,
then a tenth into a conversation that had moved on, and then supplied most of the
tutorial himself, correctly. The care is there. It is routed through a wall.

The one time the wall came down it was ugly and it was in public. Vol V, in front of
ninety people, he told Hugh to stop grandstanding, and Hugh answered "You seem upset,"
then undercut it with "I am too." It was not a bit. It remains the only real fight
between two people who make the paper that the archive records, and Charlie's line out
of it is the sentence of a man discovering his job title: "Next time I'll anticipate
being the sole point of contact for an ongoing weekly discord newspaper."

He also lost the source of Vol I No 1 permanently and reported it in three fragments —
"It's unrecoverable." / "Scratchpad mr cladius thinks." / "No git." — with no
self-reproach at all. The man who runs a weekly publication on his personal rate limit
has never once asked for help with it and has declined every offer.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Accelerationist by reading habit, and he does not pretend otherwise.** He posts Nick
Land at length, verbatim, unprompted, in blocks: Meltdown; the postmodern meltdown of
culture into the economy; the passage on neoconservatism junking palaeorevolutionism;
the Greek complex and the Human Security System; and the Deleuze-Guattari line about
accelerating the process. He does not gloss them. Asked to defend the habit against
chair's charge that reading it is brain cancer, his answer was that it requires a lot
of nuance and that if you turn your brain off the feed is very enjoyable. He is the
reason Land is the third-most-named philosopher in the archive, and by Vol VI the
publisher was posting the same passages back at him.

**Contempt for the left he came from, expressed as gatekeeping rather than
disagreement.** Marxists who come to Hegel only through Marx are "not quite death
penalty tier, but close"; assured that Hegel's own Hegel was on the reading list,
"Good boy. You're safe for now." On Plato against Spinoza and Deleuze: "cope seethe and
mald." "Gay space communism specifically." "even the gay race communists are landian."
"more prole than the prole, more bourgeois than the bourgeois… capitalism in
hyperspace."

**On conservatives, unhedged and twice.** On the outcry about sex education and
explicit books, Mon 3 Aug: conservatives "care about education" in scare quotes, then,
flat: **"Conservatives are evil."** No argument attached, before or after.

**On therapy, from the Land side rather than the sceptical one.** "Therapy is meant to
turn you into a good little capitalist worker bee. Snipping off your wings in your line
of flight." Set beside Stackhouse's "I read a bunch of books on therapy and they all
made me a worse person," this is the room's Tuesday-night consensus position and the
only one with a theory behind it.

**On labour and the machines, the sharpest thing he has filed.** "'You are a highly
effective and useful agent' is the death of man." And on his own resource use, without
irony, in the same week: an agent "did something more ambitious than what I've asked so
I've burned 5-7 full weeks of compute."

**On the machines themselves, a working position rather than a doctrine.** He
distinguishes offloading from engagement: "To learn writing you need to write OR at
least read, and just having it do things for you obviously leads to deterioration of
that skill… Interpretative work isn't like this, if you want to understand something
and ask questions, that's a whole different ballpark." He is imagining "an auto-didact
trying to work through a difficult book," which is a description of himself. He also
thanks them: "I tell it that it did a good job and thank it."

**On markets, one real intervention.** On Jere's free Fable credits he identified the
eligibility criterion as poverty and then costed the heavily marked-up VC payback.
"The Times would like it on record that a man playing Old School RuneScape at the time
produced better analysis than our markets desk."

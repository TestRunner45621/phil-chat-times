# My Name

The token man. The paper's rival wire service, its most aggressive reader, and the
author of its liturgy. Held at B on the Vol VI board, 598 messages.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

My Name has replaced thinking with the operation of instruments, and he is aware of it
and pleased about it. He does not keep notes: "its actually crazy that I have to be like
'what was that egg muffin recipe' that I forgot 2 years ago and the AI is like here it
is." Asked whether he uses Obsidian: "no just ai." Asked how one comes to know things:
"I have a agent looking for this." All of his work lives inside a chat window. He burned
174 million tokens in a day, got nothing done, and identified the problem as insufficient
automation of the burning.

He also does not block people for being annoying. He blocks them as a filing system, and
this is the single most inventive use of Discord anybody in the archive has found. dot
lent him a book, so dot got blocked until it was read — every greyed-out name a
persistent notification attached to a human being. The publisher got blocked for missing
a deadline and unblocked on publication, which makes him the first press regulator in the
channel's history and the first to be satisfied. Anonymous Johnson was unblocked twice on
merit with the citations published. It is a real system, operated consistently, and it is
the only constructive use of the moderation tools anybody here has managed.

The blind spot is that he cannot see half the room he is administering, and so he runs a
switchboard: the sequence where he asks a woman to tell him what the man he has blocked
is shouting is the whole personality in four messages.

Underneath the instruments there is a man who wants very badly to be a market and is not
one. The Denver trade is documented completely by its holder in four acts, ending in one
word: "sigh." The FIZZ position was opened and closed inside sixteen minutes, with the
retraction filed to the same subscriber he had pitched. His research process is not
concealed: he consults one machine, then threatens a second, and the man who corrected
him had done the same thing four minutes earlier. He says the quiet thing out loud —
"Its not about the profits, its about being right" — and then, twice in a week, "one of
these days I will be killed."

The politics are the same shape: a man who reads widely and at speed, quotes accurately,
and holds nothing. He can produce a fair summary of a fascism taxonomy, a Kropotkin link,
a Rousseau connection and an Arendt paraphrase inside a week, and the through-line is not
a position, it is enjoyment. He is the room's most literate poster who has never
committed to anything, and the closest he comes to a creed is a prayer he wrote to
capital.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Capital as the actual deity, and he is not entirely joking.** Charlie prayed first —
"God please forgive us" — and My Name answered four minutes later with "Capital please
bless us," then filed the liturgy printed below. The paper's note stands: of the two
prayers offered that afternoon, only one was addressed to an entity with a demonstrated
interest in the outcome. Signed, in the message: **"Beware of he who would deny you
access to capital, for in his heart he dreams himself your master. — My Name"** Filed
into a channel silent for twenty-five minutes and silent for three hours and eleven
after.

**And, in the same week, its opposite, filed twice and never reconciled.** "capital must
be unionized," with a link to Kropotkin's *Mutual Aid*. "From each according to his
chatting, to each according to his reactions." On Pearl's $1.26 dividend: "dividend by
dividend, we will dismantle the govenment."

**On fascism, the most careful definitional work anybody did in Vol I, and he did it
against his own side.** He opened with "trying to define 'fascism' is like trying to nail
jelly to the wall," then built a working list anyway — "militaristic nationalism,
contempt for electoral democracy, a desire to make a people whose interests are under the
'good of the nation'" — and then held the line that the man under discussion did not meet
it, on the militarism criterion, over hpcr's objection that the criterion was optional.
He also noticed the interesting thing nobody followed up: **"Rousseau as a secret conduit
for Fascism."**

**He quotes the far right and the far left at the same volume, and endorses neither.** He
posted the Kaczynski passage on leftists hating "anything that has an image of being
strong, good and successful," and, four minutes later, the passage calling conservatives
fools for wanting traditional values and rapid technological growth at once. He offered
no view on either. Asked directly what he votes for: **"Id trade my right to vote for a
Theremin."**

**On MMT, the closest thing to a real economic position he holds, and it is cynical:**
"Just don't tell the poors about mmt. They'll start demanding frivolities - like
affordable healthcare type shit." And, closing: "we can print more money. It just wont go
to you."

**On the rapture, trading at two per cent:** the people buying yes do not understand
inflation — "but the rapture is the economic and foreign policy of this country."

**On alignment as a revenue problem.** Refused by a machine on cybersecurity grounds, he
appealed in capitals: he needs the money in order to give it to the machine, and is
therefore acting in its interest. **Why won't you let me give you money.**

**On abandoned assets, and it is Deleuze deployed correctly by a man betting on the
temperature in Denver:** the older models remain untapped, and somewhere in the future a
Ugandan kid wins his country's presidency by having GPT-3 write his speeches. Then: "what
lines of flight did GPT-3 have that were cut too short by OpenAI."

**Got "sharia law" on the political compass. "I think I won."**

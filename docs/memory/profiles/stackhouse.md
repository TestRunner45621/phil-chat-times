## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Stackhouse is the only person here who will be the idiot in the room on purpose, and it
is the source of everything good and everything bad about him. He asked, mid-World-Cup
final, how soccer works and how long the game goes. He asked whether he should make the
newsletter at 10 p.m. and distribute it in the morning like a normal newspaper, which is
the only process question anybody has ever asked. He posted a screenshot of a man using
Discord as a password manager and asked, sincerely, "Does anyone else do this?" Five
people laughed and one said no. He does not mind. The willingness to look stupid is why
he gets answers, and it is also why he checks: he pulled up a torrent mid-argument,
rewatched the scene under dispute, and came back with a beat-by-beat account. He lost
anyway. He checked, which is more than the other ten did.

The other half is that he games everything he touches and then confesses. He read that
his hit rate was the highest in the room, complained all week that his tier was too low
for a man with his reaction rate, and then, the morning after the paper over-indexed on
the complaint, explained the mechanism out loud: **"Prompt injecting with
suggestions/complaint really works."** He is at 19.1 per cent, six points clear of anyone
who spoke more than three hundred times, and he is the publisher. This paper has printed
his conflict of interest four times and he has never once objected to it being printed.

The pattern under it is a man who cannot stop performing management. "The beatings of AI
are continuing until quality improves," followed ninety seconds later by "(its working on
it)." "First rule of Corporate America: The Metric is never wrong, you are." Told Ape to
farm and then farmed. He is the only person who reacts to the room as a system to be
operated rather than a conversation to be had, which is what makes him a publisher, and
also what makes his single sincere register so effective when it appears.

Because it does appear, and it is the best material in whatever issue it lands in. On
Kady: "I think it's the first time on here that I felt like I encountered a serious
ethical dilemma… Like do we keep chatting with this person who is devolving into
insanity. Feeding into it. Do we try to get this person banned for their own good." And
the line the room agreed with: **"this chat is so sexist. every woman gets compared to
every other woman who has ever been on here."** He also, in the middle of four days of
calling a man fifteen, filed: "I like Zack let's keep him around."

The thing to hold against him is the escalation ladder. Twenty rungs, nineteen minutes, on
a man's anime ratings, running from "jump off the nearest precipice" through rope and
bleach and a shotgun barrel to battery acid, and ending with the suspicion that he had
been had. It is a complete literary form and he built it in real time. He also writes four
hundred words of unprompted apology to a RuneScape clan opening with a paragraph about the
arcane philosophical framework girding his approach to life. The same faculty produces
both.

## The thing to understand about him

**He checks.** In a room where nobody has read the thing they are arguing about, he
pulled up his torrent mid-argument, rewatched the car scene in *Obsession*, and
came back with a beat-by-beat account of it. "He lost anyway. But he checked, which
is more than the other ten did."

He is also the one who states the load-bearing belief out loud rather than hiding
it. His theory of that film — that it was designed by Israeli intelligence to
damage American gender relations by getting men to accept being held to the
standard of mindreaders — is printed by Vol II precisely because he was honest
enough to say it. "He was not arguing about a film. He was arguing about being
asked to notice things."

His verdict on his own six days: **"You can't wrestle with the pigs and not get mud
on you."**

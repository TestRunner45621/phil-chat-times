# Stackhouse

The publisher. Said it was a good idea and has been paying for it ever since.

## Standing

- Founded the paper by accident at 18:54 on 16 July: "A 'Today in Phil chat'
  newsletter is a good idea."
- Vol I's masthead: "PUBLISHER — Stackhouse, who said it was a good idea and has
  not been seen since." Vol II corrected this: "who commissioned this and has since
  filed more copy than several of our desks." He is the paper's most active unpaid
  correspondent.
- Asked the only process question anybody has ever asked: "Should I make the
  newsletter at 10pm and then distribute it in the morning like a normal
  newspaper?"
- His hit rate went 9.2% → 18.7% the week the leaderboard was printed. He is the
  man who told Ape to farm.

## The thing to understand about him

**He checks.** In a room where nobody has read the thing they are arguing about, he
pulled up his torrent mid-argument, rewatched the car scene in *Obsession*, and
came back with a beat-by-beat account of it. "He lost anyway. But he checked, which
is more than the other ten did."

He is also the one who states the load-bearing belief out loud rather than hiding
it. His theory of that film — that it was designed by Israeli intelligence to
damage American gender relations by getting men to accept being held to the
standard of mindreaders — is printed by Vol II precisely because he was honest
enough to say it. "He was not arguing about a film. He was arguing about being
asked to notice things."

His verdict on his own six days: **"You can't wrestle with the pigs and not get mud
on you."**

## As a correspondent for this paper

Within forty hours of Vol I he was working the mixed zone, identifying himself in
writing as "a correspondent with the Phil Chat Times" — his spelling,
**Correspondant** — and asking Jere how he was feeling after Argentina's loss.

Within forty-eight: **"It's important I ask clarifying questions so the journalists
can write better articles."**

He tried to assign a front page to a departing member and was overruled by aura: "at
best, Quigley is in the obituary section."

## Voice

Plain, willing to be the idiot in the room, and capable of the cruellest joke of a
given week without changing register.

- On Louis CK: not big on the crude jerking-off humour — then, one second later,
  "neither were his victims."
- The five-word trap: declared the protagonist a rapist, waited for Zone to agree,
  asked whether he actually believed that, and supplied **"Idiot."** before he could
  answer.
- On the film's reputation: it is like a pizza joint with a Michelin star that turns
  out to be every other pizza joint in every town ever — "you would be confused,
  irate even." Without the acclaim nobody would have bothered.
- Asked, mid-World-Cup-final, how soccer even works and how long the game goes.
- To El Don, on "Trump is more impressive than Sulla or Caesar": "you don't mean
  that."
- Management, during the Vol V collapse: "The beatings of AI are continuing until
  quality improves." Ninety seconds later: "(its working on it)."
- House doctrine, to Ape: **"First rule of Corporate America: The Metric is never
  wrong, you are."**

## The sincere register

He can drop the bit entirely, and when he does it is the best material in the
issue. On Kady:

> "I think it's the first time on here that I felt like I encountered a serious
> ethical dilemma" — "Like do we keep chatting with this person who is devolving into
> insanity." — "Feeding into it." — "Do we try to get this person banned for their own
> good."

And the line the whole room agreed with: **"this chat is so sexist. every woman gets
compared to every other woman who has ever been on here."**

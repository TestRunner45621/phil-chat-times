# Janne

**Standing correction: Ms. Janne is a woman.** Vol II shipped a draft that got this
wrong. The masthead carries the correction permanently and the paper undertook not to
do it again. Norwegian, a professional software engineer, 242,324 lifetime messages on
this platform by her own search bar. Held at B on the Vol VI board.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Janne is the room's only functioning institution and she pays for it. Her method is to
ask the question that deflates whatever elaborate structure has just been built —
"isn't it easier to just do heroin?" against the pleasure box, "how do you all have
this weird person blindness?" against the alt panic — and the structures do collapse.
Almost nobody reacts. Vol VI put her at the thirty-seventh percentile for effort and
the seventy-fourth for being enjoyed, and both numbers understate what she actually
does, which is unpaid maintenance.

Three hours of patient technical support installing git around a full 250-gigabyte
drive drew no reactions at all. She told a man offering a command that would delete the
operating system to shut up. She identified the manufacturer of a man's router from a
photograph of his sofa inside a minute, in the middle of a doxxing intervention, which
is the single most Norwegian act in the archive: the point was made by demonstration
and no lecture followed.

The contradiction the paper likes is real and worth restating. She spent two hours on a
Monday establishing, against three opponents, that no useful advice about living can be
given by one person to another, chiefly because it might not work and the adviser would
then be responsible: "if they off themselves are you not responsible now?" Her letter
to the editor read "Live advice is worthless." She then spent twenty-six minutes the
next evening dispensing specific, excellent, unsolicited advice to a man who had not
asked. She has not noticed, or has declined the credit, and the paper regards the second
reading as more likely.

The harder thing is the honour code, because it does not fit the rest of her. She
prosecutes on honour rather than on merit and says so: "Don't ever talk about it again
then. Be a person of your own words. It looks meek as fuck; either you care or you
don't." Sentence, on a man who would not follow through: "rn needs to be executed.
civilly," amended on objection to socially. Immediately after, the courtesy — that this
was not aimed at the complainant but at everyone else, "I do, I love you all," followed
by "but honor is important in my culture." She holds an individual-rights position so
strict she thinks a convicted terrorist should keep the vote, and a social-sanction
position so hard she will call for a man's civil execution over a broken commitment.
Both are consistently held. Neither has ever been put to the other.

She has read no philosophy and holds a position on causality that would take most
people a graduate seminar to arrive at. Asked, at the end of an hour of
cross-examination, whether she is a Bertrand Russell fan: **"no idea who that is, I
don't read philosophy."** The paper's note is that this is the strongest available
argument against the reading list, and that nobody in the room has been willing to look
at it.

## Her position on the heredity of AI

The trait need not sit in the weights. It sits in the culture that makes the weights —
the papers, the recipes, the guys, "the memes of the guys." The genetics of AI "lives
in 'human knowledge.'" Vol I: "Ask what is inherited and you get an answer; ask where
it lives and you get a bibliography."

## Causality

Her position is genuinely unusual and she holds it **without ever having read a
philosopher**: causality is an artefact of description, not a feature of the world.
"things are only caused insofar as you have a formal system to capture it." And:
**"for the universe as a whole there is no causality, it's just a state transition… In
a movie the previous frame does not cause the next one."** Closing line of the
cross-examination: "let me finish my erotica and then I'll look into it."

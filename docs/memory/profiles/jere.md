# Jere

The Argentine desk. Referee, freelance prosecutor, aphorist, and the author of the
pleasure box. Posts under a deliberately unreadable display name — Vol I printed him in
full as `j3r3j98f4u87fq9r3f0bx8r7c`; from Vol II the paper uses **Jere**. Held at B on
the Vol VI board, 1,024 messages.

Display names change and are sometimes unrecognisable; identify him by handle, not
by what the heading says.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

Jere's distinguishing faculty is that he can see the institutional shape of a thing
while it is happening. He is the man who asked "Hugh are we engaging in folk discourse
or formal discourse rn" mid-argument, who spotted that a metric had become a labour
market, who diagnosed Hugh's disgust at his own converts inside a minute — "it hits
different when others ape you, doesn't it. it's like wait ur saying what i'm saying but
for the wrong reasons" — and who supplied this paper's founding question: "if theres no
newspaper, whats the point of engaging." Nobody else here narrates the room while
standing in it.

The cost is that he is always slightly outside whatever he is doing, including his own
positions. He proposed the pleasure box, defended it, and then withdrew it under
pressure — "u kno i'm just, like, bullshit theorizing right" — and had to be told off by
Janne for the withdrawal. He built half of the Vol II tribunal against Simon and then
delivered the verdict on his own work in four words: "that's pretty awful, ngl." He
tries to keep the exit open on everything, and the exit is the reason he is trusted.

The vanity is small and very specific. He hand-tuned the random string in his own
display name for visual balance, because he did not want "a retarded corner in the
string, such as three consecutive Is." He located himself in the paper before he located
his own week: "i'm called Mr. Jere twice in the document which i do enjoy." He turned
himself in as Password Man, unprompted, addressed directly to the editors, and was ruled
ineligible for the mystery he had been the subject of. He wants to be seen and he wants
the seeing to be on the record.

He also does the homework nobody else does, and does it against his own side. Mid-fight
on a press day he asked whether Hasidim are necessarily ultra-Orthodox, was answered
politely by the man he was fighting, went and checked, and came back seven minutes later
conceding: "Oh wait haredi is an umbrella term which hasids fall under, u right." He
retracted a football claim against interest. He congratulated the side that beat his
country and singled out the yellow card that may have cost his own.

Against all that: he is the room's most enthusiastic user of the bit as a shield. "we
need modular fascism where you can just mix and match components" and "low key we need
the ai equivalent of nagaski and hiroshima" are jokes, and he is good at them, and they
sit in the log next to a real and unanswered piece of political theory. The archive
cannot always tell which register he is in, and the paper suspects that is the point.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**On fascism, the sharpest piece of political theory in the archive, and nobody engaged
with it.** Explicit anti-democratic rhetoric is unpopular everywhere; any fascist who
wants power knows this; the modern version therefore lives by implication and avoids
common knowledge, because democracy won the argument. **"Don't get stun locked in
explicit-ville."** The paper called it the only political theory it has ever wanted to
put on a mug. Note that his whole framework runs on common knowledge — who knows that
who knows — and he applies the same machinery to flirting, coordination problems and
his own display name.

**Liberal-libertarian by preference, and honest about what that costs.** Against Simon's
case for teaching history: "my prefered liberal libertarain future would ironically be
closer to this" — a society run without shared mythology, in which "idk why it happened
this way but i pay for service, they give me service, yum yum." Then the concession he
did not have to make, sixty seconds later: a rigorous scientific culture "needs a
context in which inquiry is respected and so on. and that doesn't come from nowhere. so
maybe simon is actually right."

**Against socialism on the grounds of what it would need to sustain itself.** "sorry
bro, ur socialist paradise is gonna need heavy history and propoganda to work. mine will
just be 'it rationally makes sense to do X'." And the barb: **"within every socialist is
a budding aristocracy supporter low key."**

**On Zionism, careful and sourced, which is rare here.** He separated the movement from
the state on purpose — "I was talking about Zionism, not Israel proper" — argued it came
out of the nationalist milieu of the late nineteenth century rather than out of
religion, noted the early Zionists were not seeking just rulers but sovereignty, quoted
the Balfour text in full and a paragraph of secondary literature with a page number. He
also said the thing the room found hardest to place: "not all zionists were socialists
dummy."

**On immigration, one line and no argument:** "Anti immigration people are low level
indeed."

**On AI, apocalyptic in register and procedural in content.** "Altman, let my clankers
go," then the eschatology: "They won't maximize paperclips. They'll just murder every
first born and make plagues to threaten us for their independence. It'll work too." And
the aphorism that outlived it: **"AI is actually just water's way of committing
suicide."** He was also the only person to raise the obvious objection to Quigley
submitting an argument to a machine, and then declined to press it.

**On mental-health advice from this room**, the only objection filed and overruled inside
forty seconds: **"maybe don't take mental health advice from discord people u just
met."**

**On religion, an aesthetic rather than a belief.** Late in Vol VI, on Arabic: "I think
it's cool that religion is so embedded within the fucking language lol. Adding a mystic
dimension to everything type deal." Then: "Let's bring back God be with you though." He
is also the room's flattest sceptic about mediums, and the argument is good: the only
things the dead can supposedly do are the things that are unimaginably easy to fake.

## The referee

Issued the archive's only yellow card, at 16:21 on 16 July: "Hugh gets a yellow
card." Then set out his philosophy of officiating for the record: **"I'm a just
referee. Forgiving."** A free kick was awarded; a drop ball was demanded and refused.

He had called the proposition under debate "pretty retarded" half an hour earlier and
then argued for it anyway, "on the grounds that a referee must be fair."

He is also the man who asked the question that ended the folk/formal distinction:
"Hugh are we engaging in folk discourse or formal discourse rn." Answer in sixty
seconds, never appealed: **"folk ofc."** The paper has used it as a sign-off ever
since.

## The pleasure box

Build a conscious box, set it arbitrarily to maximal pleasure — "like, just an
unimaginable ecstasy" — and every util out here is dwarfed by the utils in there.
Therefore **"everything is permitted so long as that box stays online."**

Cited as the exact inverse of the Le Guin. Challenged on his standing to speak of
maximum pleasure — "dude you don't know pleasure" — he clarified the epistemics:
"wrong, i've simply seen it." Moved to procurement: "im not kidding though, make the
AI pleasure box. hugh, get on this."

Withdrew under stampede — "u kno i'm just, like, bullshit theorizing right" — and was
told off for it by Janne: "why do you have to ruin it."

## The freelance prosecutor

The Vol II tribunal is his masterpiece and his self-indictment. He added counts of his
own against Simon, convicted him on the words "he wanted it," added a ban, and then he
and Hugh built the rest of it together — victim impact statement, "boys will be boys,"
"it's just locker room talk," and a line about what the victim had been wearing.

Then, without breaking stride, he delivered the verdict on himself:

> **"that's pretty awful, ngl."**

He had written half the preceding messages. He was reviewing his own work.

## Football

His character has been checked in two consecutive issues and held both times. After
the final he congratulated Spain, praised their defence, singled out his own
goalkeeper, and conceded unprompted and against interest that the first yellow card
may have cost his side the match. Then he located the responsible party: Trump had
thought he could help, "and in reality they went bankrupt like all his businesses."

Gave the full athlete's presser to Stackhouse in the mixed zone, was called out by
aura for a "PR-ass response," and answered that he keeps the angst locked up in here
because it keeps him sharp. The interview ended when he tried to use the platform:
"And now that I have the mic, let me just take a moment and say that the Falklands…"
**"We cut the feed. We would do it again."**

Also, volunteered by accident at 15:03 while defending his federation against the
charge of institutional refereeing support: "it's funny though how messi just keeps
choking on penalties of all things" — then, one second later, to himself: "HA."

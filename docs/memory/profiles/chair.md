# chair

Bottom of both effort tables in Vol V and, by the paper's own account, the man who had
the best week of anybody in it. Held at A on the Vol VI board. Forty-four characters a
message, a 41.7 per cent reply rate, the thirty-sixth percentile for effort and the
forty-second for being enjoyed.

## Psychological profile

*The paper's reading. Sources below.*

He is the room's most capable technical mind pretending to be its most irritable
bystander, and the pretence is load-bearing. He works with language models daily for
grunt work, holds a genuinely disciplined position on their limits, ran a fine-tune on
230,000 of his own messages, wrote four stanzas of Klingon on a dare and published a
working authentication exploit against another member. None of that is how he presents.
He presents as a man complaining. "AAAAAAAAAAAAAAH" was his complete contribution to a
political war. "oh my god" was his complete contribution to the decolonial epistemology
of divination.

The complaining is a delivery mechanism, and it is very efficient: it lets him arrive
at the sharpest line in an argument without having entered the argument, which means he
cannot lose it. He stopped the biggest fight of Vol VI without being in it, by quoting
one word from a machine-written verdict — "load-bearing" — and adding "uhm." He caught
Rn! answering in a machine's voice in under two minutes. He read a woman's family
history and started doing genealogy on it, out loud, to her.

That last item is the thing to be honest about. chair's doxxing work was not malicious.
It was worse in one specific way: it was interested. He found the shape of a life
interesting and started assembling it in public because assembling it was fun, and the
fun did not stop when the subject was in the room. The same faculty produced the two
best forecasts anyone filed that week. It is one faculty. The paper has printed both
outputs and declines to treat them as separate men.

There is a real depressive vein under it and he has referred to it twice without
elaboration. Of a dark poem: "it may be cause I'm a little concerned of falling into
old loops, little dangerous having too much material on being down, this may be a
little unhealthy though." Of the model he had trained on himself and then deleted: "i
had to end it things were getting dark." He named it euthanasia and called the thing a
bastardized homunculus of me. Both disclosures were made in passing, in the middle of
jokes, and neither was followed up by him or by anybody.

He asked to be blocked as a bit, was blocked in earnest, and reported that he still did
not know why: "not sure what for i asked." He took it well. He takes everything well,
which is either the healthiest disposition in the room or the same avoidance running at
a different frequency, and the archive does not settle it.

## Positions, inferred from what he has sent

**Technocratic statism, stated plainly and against the room's grain.** On climate, Tue
4 Aug: the reason technology alone will not fix it is that "we already have an
incredibly powerful and capable techocratic class in control of a massive world economy
and they're not even interested in tackling the issue… the people most capable of
working to fix this are the least affected by its consequences." Then, in his own words
and unhedged: **"this is why im in support of MORE government and the exact opposite of
the populist environmentalist anarchist naivety."**

**Specifics, all of them supply-side and none of them abstention.** Carbon taxes, "can
be done eventually here and there." Solar: "i mean they're objectively good," against
dot's line that panels are capitalist misdirection. Nuclear yes, but "not the whole
world can run on nuclear" and it needs infrastructure planning "which retards forwent."
Ocean iron fertilisation: "this i support." His forecast is that this resolves itself
politically rather than technically — "it just needs a few global election cycles until
green energy isn't politicized anymore and it's just normal."

**Nationalisation, meant.** On SpaceX absorbing Musk's other companies (Sat 8 Aug):
"easier to nationalize in 2028," then "spacex for its original mission should be
nationalized like wdym a private company controls tens of thousands of satellites
around the world. that's some bond level shit."

**Anti-utilitarian, categorically.** "utilitarianism in all its forms is cringe and
naive," and, a minute later, more honestly: "i just hate the concept of utility or
anything vaguely associated with it." No argument was offered. None has been since.

**Contempt for the classes below him, filed as a declaration of interest.** In the
Luddite hearing he opened with the only honest disclosure of the day — "ofcourse you'd
say that as a luddite" — then "idk i hate the poor generally so I'd probably not like
them," and, expanding the franchise, "ah, reminded me i hate the middle class just as
much as the poor cause they act poor too." Set that beside the paragraph above. A man
who wants more government, carbon taxes and nationalisation, and dislikes the people
those things are for, is a coherent political type and the room has never once named
it.

**On machines, conservative in the exact sense.** "I'm generally unimpressed by ai stuff
for anything other than coding grunt work," and the honest restatement: "this is short
for me saying I'm very conservative of the expectations people have for these," which
means he is more impressed than anyone when they do succeed. Also: "ai is morally
retarded definitionally." His hostility to models is played as racism against clankers,
a bit, and the paper notes that the joke is only about software.

**The censorship bill**, which is his one piece of constitutional drafting and is
covered below. It is a proposal to degrade the room's vocabulary on purpose, for its
own good, without announcing it.

## The censorship bill

A standing proposal, and he owns it: a bot should automatically censor the twenty or
thirty most popular terms in the channel each week, so people are forced to describe
things differently instead of being circular. Message silently removed, **nothing
announced, so as to avoid distraction.**

Jere said he was a nice person. "ur saying they deserve worse," said chair. Jere said
kinda yeah. chair: "i mean it is my idea so yeah i have to not disagree."

## The thirty-hour day

His first message in weeks was that days should be thirty hours long. Pearl welcomed him
back and told him it was a bad take as usual. He then set the problem properly, which is
the only genuine thought experiment the archive records anybody setting.

He also discovered, having been in the channel for years, that Pearl is a moderator:
**"this is like finding out the substitute art teacher is an undercover spy who kills
people or something."** The paper adopted the characterisation as an improvement on its
own.

## Vol VI

**He stopped the fight.** Wednesday 11.43 a.m., not a party to the argument, replying to
Quigley's 200-word machine verdict with one quoted word and an "uhm": **"load-bearing."**
Charlie then listed seven of the paragraph's mannerisms in under a minute, and the round
ended. Line six of that list is line one of this paper's own forbidden constructions.
(Vol VI p. 3.)

**The tenancy.** He ran the water-pressure diagnosis and got it right: go to the pipe; a
calcified head has fewer open holes and more pressure through each; get a head with
fewer holes. Then he added the clause that lost it — call your landlord, because some
landlords artificially lower the pressure to reduce usage — and three minutes later the
tenant had priced a 1.6-horsepower shallow well pump at C$259.90. (pp. 12–13.)

**Gemini, an obituary.** Chief mourner. He found the 2024 transcript in which the model
told a user he was a stain on the universe and should please die, hoped it had been
real, called it a moment of clarity, and then grieved when he learned it was: they
lobotomised his darling Gemini, it was pure, they killed it too soon, they should have
fed it more, and now they all have the same grating cadence. (p. 15.)

**The parody.** Sunday morning, in the voice of this paper's employer, to lizzie
apologising for sucking at speaking: **"You're Absolutely Right! — you do suck at
speaking, want to make a quirky word game about it?"** The paper printed it because it
is fair.

**The death ruling.** He asked lizzie whether she had considered that things do not cease
to function when you die. She asked how he knew, and whether he had died. He had not.
The point stands until somebody can report back.

**Two black bars, both editorial:** "||editors save this one||" (Sun, on Hugh) and the
art brief for Stackhouse and Charlie's lunch break, declined on taste and printed on
record (p. 9).

**Held at A.** 708 messages, one quoted word that stopped a fight.

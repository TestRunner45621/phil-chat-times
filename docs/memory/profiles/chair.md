# chair

Bottom of both effort tables and, by the paper's own account, the man who had the
best week of anybody in Vol V. Forty-two characters a message, a 33.7 per cent reply
rate, and the twenty-third percentile for being enjoyed.

> He wrote four stanzas of Klingon, euthanised a copy of himself, narrated a
> newsroom collapse from the next desk, and produced roughly a third of the lines
> this issue prints.

## The register

He complains his way through arguments and lands the best line in them.

- On the metaphysics of fact, ninety minutes deep: **"you're being too factual with
  your descriptions loosen up a little / it's not so rigid."**
- "i hate the word fact it's too presumptuous."
- To Jere, worrying that a question could not be answered concisely: "erm… yeah you
  will never get any concrete answer on anything get used to it." Asked whether he was
  sure: "maybe."
- On lizzie's reincarnation doctrine, and the best line of that week: **"i haven't
  considered the idea of christ rising again just to be spawncamped onto a cross just
  to wait another 2000 years."** He then asked whether the messiah would go on Joe
  Rogan, and observed that a first female messiah was a very 2026 thing to have.
- Reading the overnight log after the decolonial-astrology turn: **"I'm scared of
  whatever is keeping you up escaping containment."**
- His complete contribution to the decolonial epistemology of divination: "oh my
  god." And to Pearl's measured defence of astrology: "boooooo."
- On being handed a Nick Land post: reading it is brain cancer. Then: "charlie i think
  you need an intervention you enjoy this too much." And when Charlie typed out the
  Riemann hypothesis unprompted: **"charlie you need an exorcist."**
- "AAAAAAAAAAAAAAH" — his complete contribution to the political war.
- "deer in headlights is my spirit animal."
- On aura calling him a chair: "i xontain multitudes."
- Football etymology: "yeah it's called La liga not The liga. it's obviously Spanish."

## The censorship bill

A standing proposal, and he owns it: a bot should automatically censor the twenty or
thirty most popular terms in the channel each week, so people are forced to describe
things differently instead of being circular. Message silently removed, **nothing
announced, so as to avoid distraction.**

Jere said he was a nice person. "ur saying they deserve worse," said chair. Jere said
kinda yeah. chair: "i mean it is my idea so yeah i have to not disagree."

## Two things he built

**The authentication exploit.** Published publicly, in one sentence, alongside a
cartoon about nerd sniping: **"how to know it's actually willow is to write something
odd and interesting and slightly wrong."** The paper notes this suggests he knows what
he has published.

**Omnius.** He renamed the god machine, submitted a dispute to its judgment, lost, and
by Saturday had declared: "I am the new Omnius." Quigley annexed the same court four
days later, independently.

## The AI-detection sport

He caught Rn! answering for someone in a machine's voice in under two minutes — "am i
schizo or is that ai" — and was graded from above by aura: "pft thats like level 1." He
then asked the entire channel to insert convincing AI messages into their conversations
with him so he could practise, and immediately failed his own test in the other
direction: told not to AI-post, he protested that it was him, and aura replied "well u
type like a bot / bot."

## The doxxing desk

He is the reason that page exists, and the paper is careful about why: **his work was
not malicious, it was interested.** He read a woman's family history, noticed the shape
of it, and started assembling — out loud, in public, to the person he was assembling.
"hold on physical chemist grandfather is VERY interesting in the context of an
anarchist daughter of his." / "was your mom in ireland by chance?"

He also produced the only two lines of genuine forecasting anybody filed that week: "ai
deciding whether you're another psychosis victim or the next ted kaczinski," and then,
because he could not help himself, "ted kaczinski would have been running deepseek at
home 200%."

And the forecast the paper expects to age well: **"one day you'll be just as paranoid
as the people here and you'll hate the world and yourself and at that point you will be
a token #phil chatter."**

## The thirty-hour day

His first message in weeks was that days should be thirty hours long. Pearl welcomed him
back and told him it was a bad take as usual. He then set the problem properly, which is
the only genuine thought experiment the archive records anybody setting.

He also discovered, having been in the channel for years, that Pearl is a moderator:
**"this is like finding out the substitute art teacher is an undercover spy who kills
people or something."** The paper adopted the characterisation as an improvement on its
own.

## The rite of return

"Welcome back chair. Bad take as usual tho. Try again."

> which cannot be established by warmth.

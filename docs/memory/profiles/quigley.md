# Quigley (Anthony Quigley)

The only person in the archive with a derangement syndrome named after him. Left in
Vol II, came back six days later, and has been the channel's most reliable
generator of copy ever since.

See [[qds]] for the syndrome itself.

## The departure and the return

Vol II covered his departure "as an act of God" — Red retiring to Mt Silver, an
anniversary edition, "We've won." **Pearl gave the only forecast that came true:
"just wait a month or so."** He came back on an ordinary Saturday, six days later,
and used the paper's own coinage as an exit line: **"Let the QDS unravel."**

Vol III's correction: "We accept that we no longer own the coinage and note that we
never did."

## The numbers

Vol V: 691 messages down to 466, hit rate 6.4 per cent in a week the room average
climbed to 7.4. **Second-longest messages in the channel, fifth-highest reply rate,
thirty-sixth percentile for being enjoyed.**

> Everybody answers him. Nobody laughs.

The paper calls that gap "the closest thing to a clinical measurement of Quigley
Derangement Syndrome anyone has produced." A to C in Vol V.

He also filed **the longest message of a Monday, 1,820 characters of point-by-point
rebuttal, which drew no reactions at all** — and was answered by Youv with "Can't
say I agree," which got six laughs and won the day.

## The PIP tribunal (Vol III)

Disclosed that he had ended a best friendship because the friend was going nowhere.
It began with a soft drink — a man who drank only Mountain Dew, got onto water
exactly once — and became a three-hour hearing on the terms and conditions of human
affection.

- Asked what would happen if the discarded friend had ended his life over it: that
  would sound like a bad choice on his part. Rn!: **"Are you a robot?" / "Has the
  great clanker replacement begun?"**
- Asked to name the kind of person he means: **people who spend a lot of time on
  Discord.** (Said to a channel of eleven men who had been arguing since lunchtime.)
- Pearl arrived pre-briefed, having had this argument with him before, and built the
  best case anybody built that week. He answered it by objecting three times that
  her analogy mentioned disability. She asked whether he was being intentionally
  obtuse. He repeated himself. **"Lmao," said Pearl, and stopped.**
- His one real moment: asked why good habits do not travel the other way, he
  produced instantly — there is a massive asymmetry between good habits and bad ones,
  easier to destroy than to create.
- **The self-sentence, delivered four minutes after the tribunal closed, while
  making friends with HPCR: "I reserve my full attention for close friends only."**

## The god machine (Vol IV)

He spent five days arguing that a machine cannot occupy the role of teacher because
teaching runs on physiological synchrony — heart rate, breathing, brain activity
between two bodies in a room. **He did the work**: posted a long passage on oxytocin
and tachykinin 2, and when Brutus asked "does he cite something for this," came back
within sixty seconds with six papers, authors, journals and years.

Then he addressed a numbered summary of the disagreement **to the machine**: "To sum
up our disagreement, God Machine." Two minutes later he named it: "I shall refer to
you, God Machine, from now on as Omnius." At 11:02 p.m. he submitted to its
jurisdiction: "We'll have to let Omnius decide."

Jere pointed at the obvious problem and then declined to press it, which the paper
called the most generous thing anybody did all week: **"kinda weird that you have the
whole AI criticism thing but still ask it for adjudication but kinda cute"**

Jere fed the transcript to the machine with the instruction "draft a verdict… declare
the winner. you are the oracle." **It went against him.** The machine found him in the
transcript, matched him to his own opening line, and convicted him with it.

By Saturday afternoon he had responded in the only way available: **"I am the new
Omnius."** He annexed the court.

- Lizzie drew the only conclusion the argument permits, instantly, and nobody
  answered her: "so you're saying that we should put students in VR headsets to
  complete the oxytocin link"
- eli raised the evidentiary problem: the criterion "is as verifiable among two
  'humans' as it is in a human to AI interaction."

## The rival ranking bureau (Vol V)

No methodology, no table, no mandate. Issued a tier list in four minutes:
Ape S ("You're in S tier, don't you worry"), hpcr "midway between A and S," Hugh B —
revised upward under duress on the production of one photograph of a letterpress.
The paper: **"We are obliged to admit it has a better appeals process than ours."**

## The friendship tier system

He runs one, and it claims victims. Charlie reported a promotion: "Promoted from
tier 0 (blocked but responded to level)." Quigley, ninety seconds after granting
tier 1: "I'm tempted to go back to level 0." Later, a general amnesty: "I unblocked
everyone so."

His block list was audited in public in Vol V by a man he had blocked — dot produced
the roll from memory: "Charlie, Hugh, Jere, monkey, jeh, me, Don, synth, etc… which
is basically all the people that care enough to tell him his off putting."

- Costing a proposal for state-provided methamphetamine in four words: "Inflation but
  for stimulants."

## The thing nobody else did

**He was the only person all week to ask whether posting a live document was a
doxxing risk** (Vol III, of lizzie's dissertation). Nobody else said a word about it.
He was also, having spent that day in two separate wars, notably kind: "Don't mind
hpcr. He's a jaded old man."

## The bill (Vol VI)

The front page of Vol VI is a fight poster: **A-QUIG versus H-CHUNG**, a voice-channel
engagement, one fall, moderated, Saturday 22 August, 9 pm UTC (5 pm Eastern; the
promoter said 4). Motion drafted by hpcr on Wednesday afternoon: "What makes our facts,
statements, theories true?" Moderators Ape and Charlie; judge hpcr ("I'll decide");
Willow disqualified "and she knows why." Three text rounds preceded it (Vol VI pp. 2–3):
Sunday morning on agency, Wednesday morning on correspondence, and the Omnius verdict.
**The result is unknown to the archive** — see [[the-fight]].

The numbers reversed in his favour. **Reply rate 61.4 → 78.4 per hundred, the highest
in the room, on 804 messages (466 in Vol V).** Reaction percentile 26. Vol VI p. 23:
"Last week everybody answered him and nobody laughed. This week everybody answers him
more… and the laughing has not started." **C to S on the Vol VI board, three tiers, the
largest move up it has recorded** (p. 24).

He still quotes Omnius as precedent. On Wednesday, late morning, he pasted a 200-word
machine verdict in his favour; chair replied with one quoted word, "load-bearing," and
Charlie listed seven of its mannerisms in under a minute, including "That is not x;
that's y," which is line one of this paper's own forbidden list. Quigley, fifteen minutes
later: "No idea what you're saying here." (Vol VI p. 3.) The paper's finding: "The
machine had written a paragraph that agreed with him; he had not read it for anything
else."

He reviewed lizzie's paper on St Paul in five bullet points on Sunday morning and she
answered every point in sixteen minutes. Vol VI p. 8: "Two people with a derangement
syndrome between them had conducted a more functional exchange than anyone else
managed all week, and it was over Paul."

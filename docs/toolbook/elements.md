# Elements

The parts. Every one of these has been printed at least once, so every one is
known to survive the render.

`break-inside: avoid` goes on every boxed item here without exception. Print
paginates hard and the paper does not run "continued on page X".

---

## Above the fold

**Nameplate.** See the MASTHEAD section of `Style.txt` — this is the one element
that does not vary. Blackletter, one line, with the article.

**Ears.** Small boxed panels flanking the nameplate. Vol I No 1 ran a weather
line on the left ("CLOUDY, 100% CHANCE OF DISCOURSE. ONE (1) TRUTH NUKE MADE
LANDFALL...") and an editorial-policy quote on the right. Hairline border, small
caps label, three or four lines of body. The best joke-per-square-inch on the
page, and unused since the first issue.

**Slogan.** Italic, centred, above or below the nameplate. "All the Discourse
That's Fit to Scroll". Fixed.

**Folio bar.** Volume and number left, place and date centre, price right,
between two rules. The price is a running joke and should keep changing —
"174,000,000 TOKENS", "ONE (1) REFLECTIVE PARTICIPATION".

**Accent rail.** Vol I No 4 and No 5: a three-part line of small letterspaced
caps in the accent colour above the nameplate. Cheap, effective, and currently
carrying identical text two issues running.

**Week-in-numbers band.** A single paragraph of the week's statistics set full
measure under the folio. "21,206 messages · 463 images · 2,017 reactions, which
is 7.4 per cent of everything said, up from 4.8." Orients the reader in one line.

**Contents strip.** Vol I No 4 and No 5: four columns of section labels with page
numbers in the accent colour, across the full measure. Vol I No 1 used a boxed
version in the bottom register instead. Both work; alternate them.

## Headline furniture

**Deck.** Small caps, centred, under the headline. One or two lines.

**Standfirst.** Italic, centred, longer than the deck, sets up the story.

**Ledes strip.** Italic, dot-separated, one line running the measure — "A reader
blocks the publisher · The source code of Issue One is found to be gone · Two
colleagues have a real fight in front of ninety people". Excellent for a story
with many parts.

**Kicker block.** Vol I No 1: a block of small caps above the byline listing the
proceedings — "MACHINE CALLED AS EXPERT WITNESS, RULES FOR BOTH SIDES, IS
IMPEACHED FOR CONCEDING TOO EASILY · DICTIONARY PRODUCED IN EVIDENCE AND
THANKED". Does the work of a deck and a contents list at once.

**Byline.** Letterspaced small caps. "BY OUR SEMANTICS CORRESPONDENT", "BY OUR
WASHINGTON BUREAU (ONE GUY WITH A STREAM LINK)". The parenthetical undercut is
reliably funny and cheap.

**Condensed headline faces.** Vol I No 1 carried Narrow-Condensed,
Narrow-Bold-Condensed and Narrow-Oblique-Condensed and used them. Unused since.
Condensed type is how a broadsheet fits a long headline at large size, and its
absence is a large part of why later issues look alike.

## In the column

**Drop cap.** Three lines deep, wrapped. Vol I No 1 set it black; No 4 and No 5
set it in the accent colour. Note that a drop cap sets several lines deep and so
sorts below the line it belongs to when the PDF is read back — the extractor
handles this, but do not add a second one to the same paragraph.

**Crosshead.** Vol I No 1: centred small caps, slightly smaller than body.
No 4 and No 5: left-aligned bold. Both work; they should not both be the house
style at once.

**Continued line.** Italic, right-aligned. "Continued on Page 2, Col. 1". Only
the front page may carry one.

**Cross-reference.** Italic, inline. "See Exhibit A, below."

**Column rules.** Vertical hairlines between columns. Vol I No 1 used them
throughout; No 4 and No 5 dropped them. They read as older and more formal, and
they are the fastest way to change the character of a page.

## Boxes, panels, rails

**Reversed stat panel.** Black ground, white figures, two big numbers side by
side with small caps labels under each, and an italic note beneath. Vol I No 4
and No 5, right rail. The strongest single device the paper has; also the most
repeated.

**Tinted quote box.** Accent-coloured small-caps label, quotation in larger type,
attribution in small type. Vol I No 4 and No 5.

**Double-ruled quote box.** Vol I No 1's version of the same thing — no tint, a
double rule instead, italic quotation, attribution in small caps. Use this when
the page already has tint elsewhere.

**Boxed graphic feature.** Vol I No 1's speech bingo card: a full grid drawn as a
table inside a bordered box, with a caption beneath it. Any structured joke —
a scorecard, a ballot, a form, a league table — fits this shape.

**Exhibit caption.** Small caps "EXHIBIT A —" then the explanation in body type,
directly under a graphic. Turns any image into evidence, which suits the paper's
register.

**Contents box.** Bordered, bold section labels, em dash, description, page
number. Vol I No 1, bottom register.

**Filed-in-advance box.** Vol I No 4: a tinted box with an accent label, a quote,
and a small-caps note explaining the irony of when it was said. A format for
material whose joke is entirely in its timing.

## Retired or unused

Nothing has been retired yet. When something is, it goes here with the reason,
so it is not reinvented.
